1. What this driver does

This driver makes a Modern Forms smart ceiling fan, and the light on it, ordinary Control4 devices. The fan has its six speeds, off and a direction; the light is a dimmer. Keypad buttons can be bound to twelve Button Links that belong to the driver, and a bound button's LED shows what the fan or the light is really doing. The driver talks to the fan directly over your home network and never contacts the vendor's cloud, so Control4 keeps working when the cloud is down. The fan itself has to be set up on your Wi-Fi with the Modern Forms app first (chapter 2).

  • The fan: Control4's fan with six speeds, named Level 1 to Level 6 as the stock driver names them, off, forward and reverse, and a preset speed for On and Toggle (Level 3 until you change it).
  • The light: a Control4 light with on, off and dimming. It turns on at the Default On Brightness. These lights have no colour, so there is no colour control.
  • Keypad buttons that work: Button Links for Fan Off, Fan Speed 1 to 6, Fan Toggle and Fan Cycle, and for the light's Top, Bottom and Toggle. A bound button's LED lights the moment it is pressed and is corrected if the fan does not do what was asked (chapter 5).
  • Live state: the fan reports nothing by itself, so the driver asks it, every 15 seconds and every 2 seconds for 20 seconds after any change. A change made with the remote, the wall control or the Modern Forms app shows up in Control4 within that time: on Navigators, in programming events and on the keypad LEDs.
  • A fan that goes away is handled: when the fan stops answering (power cut at the wall, Wi-Fi gone), the driver keeps the last state, says so in Status, fires the event Fan Offline and, when the fan answers again, Fan Online. It never replays old commands (chapter 7). If the fan comes back at a new address, the driver finds it there by itself and rewrites Fan Address (chapter 8).
  • Finding the fan: the driver looks for Modern Forms fans on your network by itself and lists them in Found Fans; picking one there sets the device up. It never takes a fan by itself (chapter 3).
  • One fan, checked: the driver learns the fan's identity at the first contact and sends nothing to a different fan that happens to answer at the same address, until you accept that fan with a click (chapter 3).
  • The fan's name: the driver looks up the fan's product name, for example Aviator 5, from the model number the fan reports, and shows it in Fan Model. When you click, it can name the fan item and the light item after it (chapter 3).
  • For dealers: a fan on the stock WAC driver is moved to this driver by hand in Composer, with the old device kept until the end (chapter 11).

Control4 is the source of truth. The driver reports what the fan does and never fights it: it does not re-send old state to "correct" the fan, and it does not set or clear the fan's own schedules, timers, Away mode or adaptive learning. It shows them (chapter 9) and offers two clicks that turn Away mode and adaptive learning off. Automations belong in Control4 programming, where you can see and change them. The driver never adds, moves or deletes a Control4 device and never changes keypad programming. It renames its own two items only when you click Use Selected Fan (or pick a fan in Found Fans, which does the same) or Name Items After Fan; Use Selected Fan never renames one you named yourself (chapter 3).

This is an independent driver. It is not made, endorsed or supported by Modern Forms, WAC Lighting or Snap One (Control4); the trademark notices are at the end of this page.

What appears in Composer

Adding the driver creates two items, as the stock driver does: Modern Forms Fan, the fan, and under it Modern Forms Fan Light, the light. The fan item is the main one. It carries every property and action of the driver, this Documentation tab and the fan's Preset Level. The light item shows only Control4's own light settings page (Default On Brightness, hold ramp rates, the keypad LED colours). Both use Control4's standard fan and light icons. You may rename them at any time: the driver then leaves them alone, and Name Items After Fan gives them the fan's product name whenever you want it (chapter 3). Selecting either item in Connections lists all twelve Button Links as its outputs.

Words used here: a Navigator is anything you control the house from (touch screens, remotes, the Control4 app); a link is one of the driver's Button Links; the shown speed is the speed the driver believes the fan has right now, counting a command it has just sent; Gen 3 and the like are the fan's hardware generation, which the Fan Model row names.

2. Before you start

  • The fan. A Modern Forms fan of generation 1, 2 or 3 with six speeds, already set up on your Wi-Fi with the Modern Forms app. A fan without the light kit works as a fan only. A generation 4 fan speaks a different interface and is refused with a clear Status; fans with fewer than six speeds are not supported (chapter 13).
  • The controller. Control4 OS 3.3.0 or newer. Composer Pro refuses to add the driver to an older controller ("Incompatible Driver"), and a driver that is already installed says so in Status (Controller OS 3.2.1 is older than 3.3.0; the light may misbehave). The driver uses only documented Control4 interfaces. It has been tried on OS 3.4.x only; OS 4.x has not been tried.
  • The network. The controller and the fan must reach each other on your network (the fan answers on port 80). The driver's search for fans (the Found Fans list, chapter 3) and its search for a fan whose address has changed (chapter 8) ask the network with multicast DNS, which stays on the controller's own network segment: it does not cross routers. A typed address works wherever the controller can reach the fan.
  • A fixed address (recommended). A DHCP reservation for the fan in your router is recommended, but no longer required: without one, the driver finds the fan again by itself when its address changes, usually within a minute or two, and programming sees one Fan Offline and one Fan Online (chapter 8). That needs the fan and the controller on one network segment, and the fan's answer it relies on has been seen from Gen 3 fans only. With a reservation the address never moves at all. Once the first contact has happened, the Fan ID row shows the fan's MAC address to put in the reservation.
  • One device, one controller, one asker per fan. Add one Control4 device for each fan, on one controller, and do not let anything else ask the same fan all day: the stock WAC driver (take it out of service after the move, chapter 11), another controller, an integration, or the Modern Forms app left open on a phone. These fans have a small web server, and several clients asking at once are reported to make it slow or freeze until the power is cut.

3. Adding the fan

Install and add

  1. In Composer choose Driver » Add or Update Driver or Agent…, select modernforms_fan.c4z and open it.
  2. Add Modern Forms Fan (manufacturer Modern Forms) to the room the fan hangs in, the way you add any device. The fan and its light appear in the System Design tree. Do not add a second device for the same fan.
  3. Select the fan item and open its Properties tab. Status reads Not set up: pick a fan in Found Fans, or enter Fan Address. Within about half a minute Found Fans lists the fans the driver found on the network.
  4. Point the device at the fan: pick it in Found Fans and click the row's Set (Found Fans, below), or type its address in Fan Address (next section).

Point it at the fan: Fan Address

Click in the value of Fan Address, type the fan's IPv4 address, for example 10.0.2.151, and click the row's Set button; Composer sends a changed value to the driver only then. The address is trimmed of spaces. It must be four numbers from 0 to 255 separated by dots; names such as fan.local are not accepted.

  • Status changes to Waiting for the fan at 10.0.2.151 and, within a few seconds, to Online: fan speed 3, light 40 %. Fan ID, Fan Model and Firmware fill in, and Last Action reads Learned fan MF_00005E005301 (Gen 3) at 10.0.2.151.
  • That is all it takes on a new device: the first contact pins the fan (below) with no further click.
  • The first contact leaves the names of the two items alone. To name them after the fan, click Use Selected Fan or Name Items After Fan (The names of the two items, below).
  • After adding devices choose File » Refresh Navigators so the touch screens and the app show the new tiles.
  • An empty Fan Address stops every request, the search for a fan that moved included (chapter 8), and on a device that has learned its fan also the search that fills Found Fans; Status says the device is not set up; the learned identity is kept, so typing the old address back resumes. The driver never fills an empty Fan Address in by itself. Text that is not an IPv4 address gives Fan Address "fan.local" is not an IPv4 address (the typed text cut to 24 characters), and nothing is sent until it is corrected.

Found Fans

The driver looks for Modern Forms fans on the network by itself and lists them in Found Fans, on the Properties tab. If you do not know the fan's address, pick the fan there. There is nothing to click first: the Find Fans action of 1.0 and 1.1 is gone.

  • When it looks. A few seconds after the driver starts or is updated (within about half a minute, so that several devices take turns). While the device has no fan yet, or its Fan Address is not a usable address, it looks again after 30 seconds, 1, 2, 5 and 10 minutes, then every 15 minutes. A device that has its fan looks only at start-up; while that fan is away and Follow Address Changes is On, the driver's search for it (chapter 8) refreshes the list as well. A device that has its fan and an empty Fan Address does not look at all.
  • How. One multicast DNS question, sent twice, a second apart, with 3 seconds for the answers. It is the same question the driver asks to follow its fan to a new address (chapter 8). It stays on the controller's own network segment. The driver never scans the network and never asks the internet.
  • What it never does. A search never takes a fan: it does not fill in Fan Address, send anything to a listed fan, rename the items or write Last Action. Only your pick, or an address you type, sets the device up.
  • The items after the first one read MF_Fan_005302 at 10.0.2.152: the fan's network name, which ends in the last six hex digits of its MAC address, and its address. Your router's client list shows the same MAC digits. This device's own fan comes first and reads MF_Fan_005301 at 10.0.2.151 (this device: Aviator 5), or MF_Fan_005301 at 10.0.2.151 (this device's fan) when the driver has no name for its model. The list shows at most 16 items; with more fans the last one reads ... and 3 more: enter the address in Fan Address. A fan stays listed until three searches in a row have missed it, so a fan that is silent for a moment does not drop out. The list is rewritten only when what it shows changes.

Picking a fan. Choose an item in Found Fans and click the row's Set; Composer sends the pick only then.

  • A device without a fan takes the picked fan at once. That is exactly what Use Selected Fan does (below), with the same Last Action lines, for example Now using fan MF_00005E005301 at 10.0.2.151: Fan Address is filled in, and the two items are named after the fan unless you named them yourself (The names of the two items).
  • This device's own fan is taken at once the same way: Already using fan MF_00005E005301 at 10.0.2.151, or Same fan MF_00005E005301, now at 10.0.2.152 when it is listed at another address.
  • A different fan, on a device that already has its fan, changes nothing yet. Last Action reads Picked MF_Fan_005302: click Use Selected Fan to switch to it, and the row keeps showing your pick. Click Use Selected Fan to switch the device to that fan (chapter 8). A later pick replaces it; picking the first item drops it.
  • Picking the first item or the ... more item does nothing. After a pick that takes a fan, and after every Use Selected Fan, the row shows the first item again.

The pinned identity

At the first contact the driver reads who the fan is: its client id (it starts with MF_ or WAC_ and holds the 12 hex digits of the MAC address), its MAC address and its generation. It remembers them through updates, Director restarts and reboots; the Fan ID row shows them, for example MF_00005E005301 (MAC 00:00:5e:00:53:01). From then on this device is that fan:

  • The same fan at a new address is followed: by the driver itself when the fan announces itself there and answers as itself (chapter 8), or when you type the address in Fan Address or pick it in Found Fans (or with Use Selected Fan).
  • A different Modern Forms fan answering at the address (a changed lease, a replaced fan, a typing mistake) gets nothing: no command, no state read into Control4. The device shows Another fan (...5302) answers at 10.0.2.151; nothing is sent and asks again once a minute, since the right fan may come back. Unless you typed that address yourself, it also looks for the right fan at other addresses (chapter 8). Accepting the new fan is a click (Use Selected Fan, chapter 8).
  • Nothing else is ever matched by name, model or address, and no network is scanned.

Use Selected Fan

The click that accepts a fan. The target is the pick waiting in Found Fans when there is one (a different fan picked on a device that already has its fan, above), otherwise Fan Address. The driver reads that fan's identity once and sends it nothing else. The pick is cleared after every outcome, and the row shows its first item again. A pick in Found Fans that takes a fan at once goes the same way and writes the same lines. When the click leaves a fan set up, it also looks at the names of the two items (see the endings below, and The names of the two items).

The names of the two items

The fan reports its model number, for example 1811-5-54. The driver looks that number up in a list of Modern Forms product names that comes with the driver. Only an exact match counts; the driver never guesses a name. A hit is shown in Fan Model, for example Gen 3, Aviator 5 (1811-5-54), light kit F4IN-120V-R1-30, Breeze. Naming the items means giving the fan item that name, Aviator 5, and the light item the same name followed by "Light", Aviator 5 Light. A fan without a light kit gets its light item named the same way, so a kit added later needs nothing more.

  • When. Only when you click Use Selected Fan (above; a pick in Found Fans that takes a fan counts as that click) or Name Items After Fan (below). Nothing else renames the items: not a search for fans, start-up, a driver update, a project restore, a new address typed in Fan Address (the first contact with a fan you typed in included), a firmware update, or a light kit added or removed. For a fan you typed in, click Use Selected Fan (with nothing picked it checks the typed address) or Name Items After Fan to name the items.
  • Your names stay. Use Selected Fan renames the items only while both still have the names they were created with (Modern Forms Fan and Modern Forms Fan Light) or the names the driver gave them. A name you typed, or a dealer typed, is never overwritten by it: both items stay as they are and the line ends ; names kept.
  • Name Items After Fan is the way back. It is an action on the Actions tab, a click and never programmed. It names both items after the fan whatever they are called now, and says what it did. It works from the model the driver remembers, so it also works while the fan is off or not answering. Clicking it again changes nothing and says so.
  • Two fans of one model get the same name. The driver adds no number and no room. Rename one of the items yourself: the driver then leaves it alone.
  • A model the driver has no name for gets no name: Fan Model keeps the model number (Gen 3, fan 9999-5-120, light kit F4IN-120V-R1-30, Breeze) and nothing is renamed. Note the Fan Model text and open an issue on the project's GitHub page (chapter 15); a later driver can add the name.
  • The name never comes from the Modern Forms app. The fan's name in the app is often a room name, and the driver does not even keep it (chapter 14).
  • After a rename choose File » Refresh Navigators so the touch screens and the app show the new names. A voice assistant that knows the fan by its old name needs a new discovery, and a routine that says the old name needs the new one. Anything that looks the fan up by its name, such as some third-party drivers, will not find it under the old name.

4. The fan from Control4

Speeds and Navigators

The fan is a Control4 fan with six speeds, shown as Level 1 to Level 6 (the names the stock driver used, so Navigators and programming that say "Level 2" look the same after the move), and off. A speed chosen on a Navigator, in programming or by a keypad link is sent at once as one request that turns the fan on at that speed; off is one request that turns it off. Control4's own commands are always sent, even when they equal what the driver last heard, because what it last heard can be up to one poll old. A fast series of taps ends on the last one: the requests are queued one at a time with a quarter second between them, and the newest wish wins.

On, Off, Toggle and the preset speed

  • On and Toggle (from off) turn the fan on at the preset speed, Level 3 until you change it. On a fan that already runs, On changes nothing, so a stray On never changes a speed you chose.
  • Off and Toggle (from on) turn the fan off.
  • The preset is the fan item's Preset Level in Composer (Control4's Designate Preset). The driver remembers it through updates, restarts and reboots.

Cycling

The fan proxy's own Cycle Speed Up and Cycle Speed Down step from the shown speed and stop at Level 6 and at Level 1; they never turn the fan off. The driver's Fan Cycle link for keypads works the same way upward: from off it goes to Level 1, then one speed up per press, and at Level 6 it stays at Level 6. It never turns the fan off; bind a second button to Fan Off for that.

Direction

The fan can run forward or in reverse. A direction chosen on a Navigator or in programming (set, or toggle) is written to the fan as its own request and shown in Control4's fan state; Status adds reverse while the fan runs in reverse. A change made with the remote shows up at the next poll.

Changes made elsewhere

The remote, the wall control and the Modern Forms app can change the fan at any time. The driver notices at the next poll (every 15 seconds, or sooner: after a change it asks every 2 seconds for 20 seconds), and then behaves as if Control4 had done it: the Navigator tile moves, the fan's events fire, the keypad LEDs follow, and Last Change By says who did it (chapter 9). One notification is sent per real change, and never an older one after a newer one.

What programming gets

  • The fan's own events, conditionals and variables from Control4's fan proxy appear under the fan item in Programming with Control4's wording: the fan turning on, turning off, its speed changing, its direction changing, whether it is on, off, at a given speed, or reversed.
  • The driver adds two events, Fan Offline and Fan Online, one variable, Online, and one condition, Fan is online (chapter 10). Fan Offline also fires when the wrong fan, a Gen 4 fan or a device that is not a fan answers at the address. A fan that the driver follows to a new address shows as one Fan Offline and one Fan Online; the move has no event of its own (chapter 8).
  • The light's events (on, off, level changed) are the light's own, in Programming under the light item.

Binding a button

  1. In Composer open Connections and select the keypad (or the Switch V2 or Essential Switch that is set to Use as 2 Button Keypad). The top pane lists its buttons as <button> Button Link inputs; the lower list BUTTON_LINK Output Devices lists the Modern Forms Fan's links under the fan item.
  2. Drag the button's row from the top pane onto the link's row in the lower list. (A drag that starts in the lower list does nothing.)
  3. Read both lists again: each shows the binding. The fan's Keypad Links row now counts it and names the keypad.
  4. To undo it, right-click the binding and choose Disconnect; the prompt names the two devices, not the link, so check the lists again afterwards.
Follow Connection. Making the connection switches the button's LED Behavior to Follow Connection by itself, so the LED follows the link. Disconnecting does not switch it back: set Programmed again yourself if the button's LED should obey programming once more. A button that still has programming that lights or darkens its LED ("Use the on color for…") will fight the link, because both paint the same LED: remove that programming when you bind the button (chapter 11). The driver never changes a keypad's settings.

What a press does

  • A fan link acts at once on the press. A keypad that only sends clicks is handled too: a click acts when no press arrived on that link in the previous 10 seconds. Letting go does nothing, and there is no hold and no repeat on the fan links: holding Fan Speed 3 sets Level 3 once.
  • A double tap is two presses and acts twice. For a button whose double tap should do something else, use the keypad's own Double Tap event in programming (see below): the fan may briefly go to the single-tap speed first, and the Double Tap line then sets the other speed. That relies on your keypad delivering its Double Tap event for a bound button; check it on yours.
  • Several keypads may be bound to the same link; they share it.
  • Fan offline: a press is tried once, as the check whether the fan is there, and dropped if it fails; it is never replayed. Wrong fan, Gen 4, not a fan, no address, rebooting: the press is refused and the LED stays as it was.

The LEDs

  • When you press a bound button its LED shows the target at once; when the fan answers otherwise, or the request fails, the LEDs are corrected. A change made with the remote, the app or the wall control moves the LEDs at the next poll.
  • Right after the driver starts, while the fan's state is not known yet, no LED is touched (a newly bound button is told "off"). While the fan is offline the LEDs keep their last state.
  • Colours. By default the driver sends no colours on the fan links: a bound fan button shows the on and off colours set on the keypad itself. Set Speed LED Colors to Per speed for a colour of its own at each speed (below). The light links tell the keypad the colours of the light item's settings page (blue by default; Top and Toggle lit while the light is on, Bottom lit while it is off).

Speed N Link Also Lit At

A button normally lights at its own speed only. Some houses give a button a second job through programming: a single tap sets one speed and a double tap, on the keypad's own Double Tap event, sets another. Without help that button would go dark at the second speed. Each speed link therefore has a row on the fan item, Speed 1 Link Also Lit At to Speed 6 Link Also Lit At, with the choices No other speed (the default) and Speed 1 to Speed 6. The link's LED is then lit while the fan runs at its own speed or at that one.

Example. A button is bound to Fan Speed 2 and its Double Tap event sets Level 1. Set Speed 2 Link Also Lit At to Speed 1 and click Set: the button is lit at Level 2 and at Level 1. This is an LED rule only; the double tap itself stays keypad programming. Choosing a link's own speed means the same as No other speed. A change of the row is applied to the LED at once.

Speed LED Colors: a colour per speed

A button on Follow Connection has one on colour, the one set on the keypad. To show a different colour at each speed (for example a button lit blue at Level 1 and orange at Level 6), set Speed LED Colors to Per speed and pick the colours: Speed 1 LED Color to Speed 6 LED Color are the colours a speed link shows while the fan runs at that speed, and Speed LED Off Color is the colour it shows when dark. Each is a Composer colour row: its R, G and B boxes only show the value. Click the colour swatch, choose Define Custom Colors, type Red, Green and Blue (for example 0, 0 and 255 for blue), click OK, then click Set on the row. A row already at that colour shows no Set. Set the colours first and switch Speed LED Colors to Per speed last, so the buttons never show the default blue in between. With Keypad's own (the default) the driver sends no colours and the six colour rows do nothing.

The driver sends the colours over the link, just before it lights the button, and again whenever you change a colour while the button is lit. A button that is bound later, or a keypad that asks, gets them too. Going back to Keypad's own sends nothing: the keypad keeps the last colours it took until you set its own on the keypad. The Off, Toggle and Cycle links and the light's links are not affected.

Up and Down buttons

A keypad's Up and Down buttons are often Regular Buttons without an LED, but they have Button Link inputs too. Bind Up to Light Top and Down to Light Bottom: a tap turns the light on (at the Default On Brightness) or off, as a programmed line would, and a hold now brightens (Up) or dims (Down) it. These buttons have no LED to follow.

The Keypad Links row

Keypad Links lists the bindings: the first item reads 5 of 12 links bound, then one item per binding, for example Fan Speed 3: Example Keypad (456) (the link without "Button Link", the keypad's name and its device id). Picking an item changes nothing.

6. The light

The light is an ordinary Control4 dimmer with on, off and a level from 1 to 100 %. The fan keeps its light's level while the light is off. There is no colour and no colour temperature. Its settings page (select the light item) is Control4's own.

Turning on and the Default On Brightness

A tap on a Top button, a tap on a Toggle button while the light is off, Control4's On command and a Navigator's on all turn the light on at the Default On Brightness, 100 % until you change it on the light item's settings page (1 to 100). A level chosen with the slider or in programming is exactly that level. To turn the light on at a level other than the one the fan kept, the driver first sets the level and then switches the light on, so the light never flashes at its old level; that is two requests, one after the other.

No fades

The fan's light cannot fade. Every level is sent at once, and a ramp or fade time that Control4 supplies (including the settings page's default rate) is ignored. A slider drag is not sent step by step: while one request is on its way only the newest level is kept, so the light follows the slider in a few jumps and ends where the slider ends.

Holds

On a bound keypad button a press held longer than 0.4 seconds is a hold. The driver steps the light's level at most twice a second, so the light changes in small steps rather than smoothly:

  • Top (or Toggle while the light is off) brightens toward 100 %; Bottom (or Toggle while the light is on) dims toward 1 %. The whole range takes 5 seconds unless you change the Hold Ramp Rates on the settings page, at that speed from any level, and it stops where you let go.
  • Held while the light is off, Top or Toggle turns it on at 1 % and brightens from there (about 0.6 seconds later). Bottom does nothing while the light is off.
  • A tap that arrives within a second of letting go of a hold on the same button is ignored, because some keypads send one.

Advanced Lighting Scenes

The light can be a member of Control4's Advanced Lighting Scenes. A scene's level is set at once. Ramp-up and ramp-down commands of a scene start a hold-style ramp and the stop commands freeze it; these have not been tried on a real scene yet.

No light kit, and a fan that is away

A fan without a light kit shows no light kit in Status and Fan Model. The light item then stays unavailable and its commands are refused and logged. While the fan is offline, the light's controls are greyed out on Navigators; coming back, they return.

Offline

After a failed request the driver tries again after 10 seconds, then 20, then 40 and then once a minute; the third failure in a row makes the fan offline, about 45 seconds after it went quiet. It cannot tell a power cut at the wall from a stuck Wi-Fi module while the blades keep turning, and it does not guess: it keeps the last state, marked not current in Status, instead of reporting the fan as off. Control4's fan has no "offline" of its own, so the fan tile keeps showing the last speed; use Status, the Online variable and the Fan Offline event, and the light's greyed-out controls. While the fan is offline the driver also looks for it on the network by its MAC address, in case its address has changed (chapter 8).

Coming back

The first answer after the silence starts a new first contact: the driver checks the fan's identity and firmware, reads its state and reports what differs from the last known state. Fan Online fires once and the fast polling (every 2 seconds for 20 seconds) starts. Nothing is re-sent, whatever Control4 last commanded: a fan stopped with the wall control's pull tab stays stopped, and a speed you sent while it was away is not delivered late.

Reboot Fan

For a Wi-Fi module that has stopped behaving, Reboot Fan (Actions tab) asks the fan to restart its Wi-Fi module. It works only while the fan answers, only on your click, and never automatically; it is not a programming command. The driver then waits up to two minutes (Status Rebooting the fan's Wi-Fi module (sent 14:02)), asking every 10 seconds; the first answer starts a first contact. After two minutes of silence the fan counts as offline. Last Action says:

  • Reboot sent to the fan's Wi-Fi module at 14:02
  • Reboot not sent: the fan is not answering (power it off for 10 s) while the fan is offline: a fan that does not answer cannot hear it; switch its power off at the wall for 10 seconds instead.
  • Reboot not sent: no fan of this device answers at 10.0.2.151 when no address is set or another fan, a Gen 4 fan or a non-fan answers.
  • Reboot already sent at 14:02; waiting for the fan while it is rebooting.

The wrong fan

The driver pins the first fan it meets. If a different Modern Forms fan answers at the address (a new lease handed the address to another fan, a fan was replaced, the wrong address was typed), the driver refuses to talk to it: Status reads Another fan (...5302) answers at 10.0.2.151; nothing is sent, commands are refused, keypad LEDs keep their state and Fan Offline fires. The pinned fan is not forgotten; the driver asks again every minute and carries on by itself if the right fan returns to that address, or, unless you typed that address yourself, follows it if it turns up at a new one (chapter 8). To use the other fan on purpose, see chapter 8.

Following a new address

Once the driver has learned the fan (chapter 3), the device belongs to that fan, not to an address. When the fan stops answering at Fan Address, or something else answers there, the driver asks the network for the fan by its MAC address, with the same multicast DNS question that fills Found Fans (chapter 3). If the fan announces itself at another address, the driver reads its identity there once, sends it nothing else, and moves only when the client id and the MAC address are the learned fan's. No click is needed.

  • When. The search starts when the driver counts the fan offline, about 45 seconds after it went quiet (three failed requests), or at once when another fan, a Gen 4 fan or a device that is not a fan answers at the address. The first look is 2 seconds later, plus up to about 25 seconds so that the devices of several fans do not all ask at the same moment; then after 30 seconds, 1, 2, 5 and 10 minutes, then every 15 minutes while the fan is away. Each look takes a few seconds. A fan that has moved is usually found again within a minute or two of going quiet. This search does not run while the fan answers, while it reboots, or while Fan Address is empty (the search at start-up that fills Found Fans is chapter 3's).
  • What you see. Status goes from Not answering at 10.0.2.151 since 14:02; showing last state to Waiting for the fan at 10.0.2.152 and then Online: …. Fan Address is rewritten with the new address, and Last Action reads Followed fan MF_00005E005301 to 10.0.2.152 (was 10.0.2.151) (also written to the log). Programming sees one Fan Offline and one Fan Online, as for any outage; the move has no event of its own, and nothing is re-sent to the fan. The device, its links, its programming and the names of its two items stay as they are. Each of these searches also refreshes Found Fans, which then lists the fan at its new address, but the driver never takes anything from that list by itself. A look that finds nothing changes nothing and writes nothing in Last Action.
  • An address you type wins. After you click Set on Fan Address, the driver follows the fan nowhere else until the fan has answered at the address you typed. A typing mistake, another fan's address or an address for later therefore stays as you typed it, and Status says what answers there. Typing the address the fan was last seen at is not such a change: the driver carries on as before. An empty Fan Address stops everything, following included, and the driver never fills it in.
  • Only this fan, only nearby. The driver follows only the fan it has learned: never another fan, never by name or model, and only to a private or link-local address (the kind a home network uses) that the fan itself announced. It never scans the network. Multicast DNS stays on the controller's own network segment, so a fan on another subnet, VLAN or guest Wi-Fi, or behind an access point that filters multicast, is not found; type its address instead.
  • Too many moves. When the fan would move a fourth time within an hour, the driver does not follow it, says so once (below) and follows again once the hour is over. An address that changes that often, or two devices answering for one fan, needs a look at the router.
  • Switching it off. Set Follow Address Changes to Off and click Set (chapter 10). A fan at a new address then stays "not answering" until you type its address. The driver then searches only at start-up, so Found Fans shows the fan at its new address only after a restart or an update.
  • Tried so far. The fan's answer that following relies on has been seen from Gen 3 fans only. Gen 1 and 2 fans are expected to answer the same way, but that has not been tried.

9. The fan's own automations

These fans can run things themselves: schedules and sleep timers set in the Modern Forms app, Away mode (random on and off), adaptive learning. The driver does not fight them or hide them. It shows them, says when a change was not made by Control4, and offers two clicks.

  • Fan Automations reads None, or Active: and the items that apply, joined by commas: schedule, away mode, adaptive learning, fan timer 25 min, light timer 10 min (Gen 3 fans report the time left; minutes are rounded up) or fan timer set, light timer set (Gen 1 and 2). It is rewritten when the text changes.
  • Last Change By is written whenever the fan changed and Control4 did not do it, for example Remote or wall control at 14:05. Gen 3 fans say where a change came from, and the driver words it: Remote or wall control, Modern Forms app or another local client, Modern Forms cloud (app away, voice assistant), The fan's sleep timer, The fan's away mode, A schedule in the Modern Forms app. When the fan does not say (Gen 1 and 2 fans, or a source it does not name), it reads Outside Control4 (source not reported). What a given remote reports has not been checked on every model.
  • Turn Off Away Mode and Turn Off Adaptive Learning (Actions tab) switch those two off. They never switch them on; the Modern Forms app can turn them on again. Last Action reads Away mode turned off (the Modern Forms app can turn it on again) or Adaptive learning turned off (the Modern Forms app can turn it on); Away mode is already off or Adaptive learning is already off if nothing was needed; Not changed: the fan is not answering; and The fan did not take it; it still reports away mode on (… adaptive learning on) if the fan refused.
  • Schedules and timers are never set or cleared by the driver. Clear them in the Modern Forms app, and put the automation in Control4 programming instead, where it can be seen with the rest of the house.

11. Moving from the stock WAC driver

A Control4 device cannot be switched to another driver, and this driver never takes over from the stock one (wac_mf_fan_and_light_controller(v4).c4z) by itself. The move is done by hand in Composer; no action of the driver does it or reports on it. Back up the project first. The stock device stays until the last step, so every step before that can be undone.

  1. Add this driver's device beside the stock one, in the fan's room (chapter 3). While both exist, give the two new items names you can tell apart, for example "Fan (new)" and "Fan Light (new)". The driver keeps names you typed; do not click Name Items After Fan unless you want the fan's product name instead.
  2. Find and use the fan: pick the fan in Found Fans, which the driver fills by itself, and click Set; or type its address in Fan Address. Go on once Status reads Online: ….
  3. Move each keypad button. First take the button away from the stock device: disable the programming lines that send its presses to the stock fan or light, or disconnect it from a stock link it is bound to. Then bind it to this driver's link (chapter 5), so that a press never reaches the fan through both drivers. Disable the programming that lights or darkens the button's LED as well: it names only the keypad, so deleting the stock device would not remove it, and it fights the link. A double tap stays programming: add a line on the button's own Double Tap event, aimed at the new fan, and set Speed N Link Also Lit At so that the button stays lit at that speed (chapter 5).
  4. Move every other line of programming that commands or tests the stock fan or its light, in scripts, timers, scheduler events and macros: aim it at the new fan or light, or disable it. A script on one of the stock items' own events (such as one that runs when its speed changes) is disabled, and rebuilt on the new item's event if it is still needed. In Programming, go through the events of the stock device's two items, of the keypads and of the agents. Outside programming, check Advanced Lighting Scenes, Navigator favourites and voice assistants' routines that name the fan.
  5. Check it. Press each moved button and watch the fan and the LED, and use the fan and the light on a Navigator. Until the next step, a disabled line can be enabled again and a binding disconnected.
  6. Remove the old setup. Delete the old lines you disabled (the LED lines too), then the stock device. Rename the new items to the names the family knows, choose File » Refresh Navigators and back up the project again. Do not keep both devices longer than needed: two drivers asking one fan can make it slow or freeze it (chapter 2).

Never delete and re-add this driver's device once buttons are bound to it (chapter 8). One house's move, step by step and with a way back from each step, is recorded on GitHub.

Things that look wrong but are not

  • The fan tile keeps its last speed while the fan is offline. Control4's fan has no offline state; Status and the Online variable carry it.
  • On a running fan, On does nothing. That is deliberate (chapter 4).
  • A light that is on is never shown at 0 %: its lowest level is 1 %.
  • For a few seconds after every update, restart or address change Status reads Waiting for the fan at… while the driver checks which fan answers.
  • Fan Address can change without anyone typing it: the driver followed the fan to its new address, and Last Action says so (chapter 8).
  • Found Fans changes without anyone clicking, and Last Action does not say so: the driver keeps the list current by itself (chapter 3).

The log

With Debug Mode On, the fan item's Lua tab shows a line for each request and answer, each message to the proxies and each LED message (open the tab first: Composer shows only lines that arrive while it is open). Without it, only warnings and errors appear, for example fan #43 failed: timeout after 5.0 s (1 in a row), Fan offline: 3 failures since 14:02, Fan online again after 3 min, Another fan (...5302) answers at 10.0.2.151 or The fan did not take speed 3 (reports 2). A change in the fans found is written as well, for example Found Fans: 2 fans (...5301 at 10.0.2.151, ...5302 at 10.0.2.152). The log never holds the owner's e-mail address, the Wi-Fi name or any other private field (chapter 14); the client id and MAC address appear only as their last four digits. Turn Debug Mode Off again afterwards.

13. Limits

  • Fans: Modern Forms generations 1 to 3 with six speeds. A generation 4 fan is refused; a fan with fewer speeds is not supported. One device controls one fan; several fans are several devices.
  • No push: the fan reports nothing, so everything made outside Control4 is seen at the next poll (15 seconds by default).
  • The fan's own automations: schedules, sleep timers, Away mode and adaptive learning are shown but never set or cleared, apart from the two turn-off clicks. Breeze, UV-C and other special features are not controlled; Breeze is only named in Status.
  • No fades and no colour on the light; no colour temperature.
  • Address: only a dotted IPv4 address, no host names. The driver follows the fan it has learned to a new address only when the fan announces itself on the controller's own network segment, only to a private or link-local address, and only after the fan has answered there as itself; an address you typed wins until the fan has answered at it (chapter 8). It never scans the network and never takes another fan by itself. The search that fills Found Fans asks one multicast DNS question on the controller's own network segment, by itself: at every start-up, and on a schedule while the device has no fan yet. Following relies on an answer seen from Gen 3 fans only so far.
  • Keypads: a bound fan button shows the keypad's own LED colours unless Speed LED Colors is Per speed. Double taps are keypad programming. No hold on the fan links.
  • Programming: two events and one condition of the driver's own, no commands of its own (a Reboot Fan or a turn-off is a click, never programmed), no "changed outside Control4" event, no History entries.
  • Icons: the driver's own fan and light icons. A device keeps the icons of the build that created it, so a device added with build 73 or earlier keeps Control4's own fan and light icons after an update; only removing and adding the device again would change them, and that loses its connections and programming.
  • Detecting a second device for the same fan: not done; add one device per fan.
  • A restored project: the identity is expected to be found again from Fan Address and Fan ID and checked at the first contact, and a fan that moved since the backup to be followed (chapter 8); the light's keypad settings may fall back to their defaults. This has not been tried yet.
  • Controllers: Control4 OS 3.3.0 or newer. Tried on OS 3.4.x only.

14. Privacy

The driver talks only to the fan on your network and to nothing outside the house. To find fans (the Found Fans list, chapter 3, and following a fan that moved, chapter 8) it also asks, by itself, one multicast DNS question that stays on your local network: at every start-up, and on a schedule while the device has no fan yet or is following its fan. It never scans the network. It reads the fan's state and identity: on, speed, direction, light on and level, Breeze, Away mode, adaptive learning, whether a schedule or a timer is set, the model, the firmware, the client id and the MAC address.

The fan also hands out details that belong to you, and the driver drops them the moment they arrive: the owner's e-mail address, the cloud identity, the fan's name from the app, the Wi-Fi network's name and address, the time zone, the cloud certificate and the schedule itself (it only notes whether one exists). They are never stored, never shown in a property and never logged. This differs from the stock driver, whose rows show some of them.

The client id and MAC address are shown in the Fan ID row, in your own Composer, because you need them for the router; in the log they appear only as their last four digits. Before you share a screenshot of the Properties tab, hide Fan ID and Fan Address.
