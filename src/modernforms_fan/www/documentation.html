1. What this driver does

This driver makes a Modern Forms smart ceiling fan, and the light on it, ordinary Control4 devices. The fan has its six speeds, off and a direction; the light is a dimmer. On a fan that has Breeze, Breeze can be switched from Control4 as well. Keypad buttons can be bound to thirteen Button Links that belong to the driver, and a bound button's LED shows what the fan or the light is really doing. The driver talks to the fan directly over your home network and never contacts the vendor's cloud, so Control4 keeps working when the cloud is down. The fan itself has to be set up on your Wi-Fi with the Modern Forms app first (chapter 2).

  • The fan: Control4's fan with six speeds, named Level 1 to Level 6 as the stock driver names them, off, forward and reverse, and a preset speed for On and Toggle (Level 3 until you change it; chapter 5).
  • The light: a Control4 light with on, off and dimming. It turns on at the Default On Brightness, and it fades: a keypad tap, a hold and a ramp in programming take the time Control4's settings give them, in small steps, and a second tap stops a fade where it is (Fades, chapter 6). For a light kit that stays dark at its lowest levels, Light Min On makes Control4's 1 % the first level that lights, as Min On does on a Control4 dimmer (Light Min On, chapter 6). These lights have no colour, so there is no colour control.
  • Breeze: on a Gen 3 fan that has Breeze, the fan's mode that varies its speed like a natural breeze, programming can switch Breeze and set its strength, programming events and a condition follow it, and a keypad button can toggle it with an LED that shows it. The fan runs either in its normal fan mode or in Breeze, never both, so the fan item shows fan mode only, and the Navigators get a second fan beside it, the Breeze fan, with the three speeds Breeze 1, Breeze 2 and Breeze 3; one click adds it (Breeze, chapter 8, and The Breeze fan, chapter 9).
  • Keypad buttons that work: Button Links for Fan Off, Fan Speed 1 to 6, Fan Toggle, Fan Cycle and Fan Breeze, and for the light's Top, Bottom and Toggle. A bound button's LED lights the moment it is pressed and is corrected if the fan does not do what was asked; two animations of the speed buttons' LEDs can be turned on, gusts while the fan blows in Breeze and a short chase in the speed's colour at a fixed speed (chapters 7 and 10).
  • Live state: the fan reports nothing by itself, so the driver asks it, every 15 seconds and every 2 seconds for 20 seconds after any change. A change made with the remote, the wall control or the Modern Forms app shows up in Control4 within that time: on Navigators, in programming events and on the keypad LEDs (chapter 5).
  • A fan that goes away is handled: when the fan stops answering (power cut at the wall, Wi-Fi gone), the driver keeps the last state, says so in Status, fires the event Fan Offline and, when the fan answers again, Fan Online. It never replays old commands (chapter 11). If the fan comes back at a new address, the driver finds it there by itself and rewrites Fan Address (chapter 12).
  • Finding the fan: the driver looks for Modern Forms fans on your network by itself and lists them in Found Fans; picking one there sets the device up. It never takes a fan by itself (chapter 3).
  • One fan, checked: the driver learns the fan's identity at the first contact and sends nothing to a different fan that happens to answer at the same address, until you accept that fan with a click (chapter 3).
  • The fan's name: the driver looks up the fan's product name, for example Aviator 5, from the model number the fan reports, and shows it in Fan Model. When you click, it can name the fan item and the light item after it (chapter 3).
  • For dealers: a fan on the stock WAC driver is moved to this driver by hand in Composer, with the old device kept until the end (chapter 4).

Control4 is the source of truth. The driver reports what the fan does and never fights it: it does not re-send old state to "correct" the fan, and it does not set or clear the fan's own schedules, timers, Away mode or adaptive learning. It shows them (chapter 13) and offers two clicks that turn Away mode and adaptive learning off. Automations belong in Control4 programming, where you can see and change them. The driver adds a Control4 device only when you click Add Breeze Fan (chapter 9), never moves or deletes one, and never changes keypad programming. It renames its own two items only when you click Use Selected Fan (or pick a fan in Found Fans, which does the same) or Name Items After Fan; Use Selected Fan never renames one you named yourself (chapter 3).

This is an independent driver. It is not made, endorsed or supported by Modern Forms, WAC Lighting or Snap One (Control4); the trademark notices are at the end of this page.

What appears in Composer

Adding the driver creates two items, as the stock driver does: Modern Forms Fan, the fan, and under it Modern Forms Fan Light, the light. The fan item is the main one. It carries every property and action of the driver, this Documentation tab and the fan's Preset Level. The light item shows only Control4's own light settings page (Default On Brightness, Default Brightness Rate, the click rates, the hold ramp rates, the keypad LED colours). Both carry the driver's own fan and light icons (a device added before release 1.1 keeps Control4's, chapter 16). You may rename them at any time: the driver then leaves them alone, and Name Items After Fan gives them the fan's product name whenever you want it (chapter 3). Selecting either item in Connections lists all thirteen Button Links as its outputs. Connections also shows the output Breeze Switch, which the Breeze fan is connected to (the output keeps the name it had in release 1.2, when the Breeze companion was a switch), and the input Ambient Light Level (Follower), which a keypad's Ambient Light Level (Source) can be connected to so that the keypad LED animations pause while that keypad is dark (Pausing the animations in the dark, chapter 10).

A third item appears only when you click Add Breeze Fan: the Breeze fan, for example Aviator 5 Breeze, in the fan's room. It comes from a second driver, modernforms_breeze_fan.c4z (Modern Forms Breeze Fan), which is installed together with this one (The Breeze fan, chapter 9).

Words used here: a Navigator is anything you control the house from (touch screens, remotes, the Control4 app); a link is one of the driver's Button Links; the shown speed is the speed the driver believes the fan has right now, counting a command it has just sent; fan mode and Breeze mode are the two ways the fan's motor can run (Breeze, chapter 8); Gen 3 and the like are the fan's hardware generation, which the Fan Model row names.

2. Before you start

  • The fan. A Modern Forms fan of generation 1, 2 or 3 with six speeds, already set up on your Wi-Fi with the Modern Forms app. A fan without the light kit works as a fan only. A generation 4 fan speaks a different interface and is refused with a clear Status; fans with fewer than six speeds are not supported (chapter 16).
  • The controller. Control4 OS 3.3.0 or newer. Composer Pro refuses to add the driver to an older controller ("Incompatible Driver"), and a driver that is already installed says so in Status (Controller OS 3.2.1 is older than 3.3.0; the light may misbehave). The driver uses only documented Control4 interfaces, with one exception that you opt into: the Ambient Light Level (Follower) input, which Control4 does not document for drivers (Pausing the animations in the dark, chapter 10). It has been tried on OS 3.4.x only; OS 4.x has not been tried.
  • The network. The controller and the fan must reach each other on your network (the fan answers on port 80). The driver's search for fans (the Found Fans list, chapter 3) and its search for a fan whose address has changed (chapter 12) ask the network with multicast DNS, which stays on the controller's own network segment: it does not cross routers. A typed address works wherever the controller can reach the fan.
  • A fixed address (recommended). A DHCP reservation for the fan in your router is recommended, but no longer required: without one, the driver finds the fan again by itself when its address changes, usually within a minute or two, and programming sees one Fan Offline and one Fan Online (chapter 12). That needs the fan and the controller on one network segment, and the fan's answer it relies on has been seen from Gen 3 fans only. With a reservation the address never moves at all. Once the first contact has happened, the Fan ID row shows the fan's MAC address to put in the reservation.
  • One device, one controller, one asker per fan. Add one Control4 device for each fan, on one controller, and do not let anything else ask the same fan all day: the stock WAC driver (take it out of service after the move, chapter 4), another controller, an integration, or the Modern Forms app left open on a phone. These fans have a small web server, and several clients asking at once are reported to make it slow or freeze until the power is cut.

3. Installing, updating and setting up

Install the release's two packages, add one device for the fan, and point the device at the fan, by picking it in Found Fans or by typing its address. The sections after the steps explain each part: the address, the list of fans the driver finds, the fan's identity, the names of the two items, and updates.

Install and add

  1. In Composer choose Driver » Add or Update Driver or Agent…, select both files of the release, modernforms_fan.c4z and modernforms_breeze_fan.c4z (the Breeze fan, chapter 9), and open them. An update goes the same way (Updating the driver, below).
  2. Add Modern Forms Fan (manufacturer Modern Forms) to the room the fan hangs in, the way you add any device. The fan and its light appear in the System Design tree. Do not add a second device for the same fan.
  3. Select the fan item and open its Properties tab. Status reads Not set up: pick a fan in Found Fans, or enter Fan Address. Within about half a minute Found Fans lists the fans the driver found on the network.
  4. Point the device at the fan: pick it in Found Fans and click the row's Set (Found Fans, below), or type its address in Fan Address (next section).

Point it at the fan: Fan Address

Click in the value of Fan Address, type the fan's IPv4 address, for example 10.0.2.151, and click the row's Set button; Composer sends a changed value to the driver only then. The address is trimmed of spaces. It must be four numbers from 0 to 255 separated by dots; names such as fan.local are not accepted.

  • Status changes to Waiting for the fan at 10.0.2.151 and, within a few seconds, to Online: fan speed 3, light 40 %. Fan ID, Fan Model and Firmware fill in, and Last Action reads Learned fan MF_00005E005301 (Gen 3) at 10.0.2.151.
  • That is all it takes on a new device: the first contact pins the fan (below) with no further click.
  • The first contact leaves the names of the two items alone. To name them after the fan, click Use Selected Fan or Name Items After Fan (The names of the two items, below).
  • After adding devices choose File » Refresh Navigators so the touch screens and the app show the new tiles.
  • An empty Fan Address stops every request, the search for a fan that moved included (chapter 12), and on a device that has learned its fan also the search that fills Found Fans; Status says the device is not set up; the learned identity is kept, so typing the old address back resumes. The driver never fills an empty Fan Address in by itself. Text that is not an IPv4 address gives Fan Address "fan.local" is not an IPv4 address (the typed text cut to 24 characters), and nothing is sent until it is corrected.

Found Fans

The driver looks for Modern Forms fans on the network by itself and lists them in Found Fans, on the Properties tab. If you do not know the fan's address, pick the fan there. There is nothing to click first: the Find Fans action of 1.0 and 1.1 is gone.

  • When it looks. A few seconds after the driver starts or is updated (within about half a minute, so that several devices take turns). While the device has no fan yet, or its Fan Address is not a usable address, it looks again after 30 seconds, 1, 2, 5 and 10 minutes, then every 15 minutes. A device that has its fan looks only at start-up; while that fan is away and Follow Address Changes is On, the driver's search for it (chapter 12) refreshes the list as well. A device that has its fan and an empty Fan Address does not look at all.
  • How. One multicast DNS question, sent twice, a second apart, with 3 seconds for the answers. It is the same question the driver asks to follow its fan to a new address (chapter 12). It stays on the controller's own network segment. The driver never scans the network and never asks the internet.
  • What it never does. A search never takes a fan: it does not fill in Fan Address, send anything to a listed fan, rename the items or write Last Action. Only your pick, or an address you type, sets the device up.
  • The items after the first one read MF_Fan_005302 at 10.0.2.152: the fan's network name, which ends in the last six hex digits of its MAC address, and its address. Your router's client list shows the same MAC digits. This device's own fan comes first and reads MF_Fan_005301 at 10.0.2.151 (this device: Aviator 5), or MF_Fan_005301 at 10.0.2.151 (this device's fan) when the driver has no name for its model. The list shows at most 16 items; with more fans the last one reads ... and 3 more: enter the address in Fan Address. A fan stays listed until three searches in a row have missed it, so a fan that is silent for a moment does not drop out. The list is rewritten only when what it shows changes.

The pinned identity

At the first contact the driver reads who the fan is: its client id (it starts with MF_ or WAC_ and holds the 12 hex digits of the MAC address), its MAC address and its generation. It remembers them through updates, Director restarts and reboots; the Fan ID row shows them, for example MF_00005E005301 (MAC 00:00:5e:00:53:01). From then on this device is that fan:

  • The same fan at a new address is followed: by the driver itself when the fan announces itself there and answers as itself (chapter 12), or when you type the address in Fan Address or pick it in Found Fans (or with Use Selected Fan).
  • A different Modern Forms fan answering at the address (a changed lease, a replaced fan, a typing mistake) gets nothing: no command, no state read into Control4. The device shows Another fan (...5302) answers at 10.0.2.151; nothing is sent and asks again once a minute, since the right fan may come back. Unless you typed that address yourself, it also looks for the right fan at other addresses (chapter 12). Accepting the new fan is a click (Use Selected Fan, chapter 12).
  • Nothing else is ever matched by name, model or address, and no network is scanned.

A pick in Found Fans that takes a fan at once goes the same way and writes the same lines. The names of the two items

The fan reports its model number, for example 1811-5-54. The driver looks that number up in a list of Modern Forms product names that comes with the driver. Only an exact match counts; the driver never guesses a name. A hit is shown in Fan Model, for example Gen 3, Aviator 5 (1811-5-54), light kit F4IN-120V-R1-30, Breeze. Naming the items means giving the fan item that name, Aviator 5, and the light item the same name followed by "Light", Aviator 5 Light. A fan without a light kit gets its light item named the same way, so a kit added later needs nothing more.

  • When. Only when you click Use Selected Fan (above; a pick in Found Fans that takes a fan counts as that click) or Name Items After Fan (below). Nothing else renames the items: not a search for fans, start-up, a driver update, a project restore, a new address typed in Fan Address (the first contact with a fan you typed in included), a firmware update, or a light kit added or removed. For a fan you typed in, click Use Selected Fan (with nothing picked it checks the typed address) or Name Items After Fan to name the items.
  • Your names stay. Use Selected Fan renames the items only while both still have the names they were created with (Modern Forms Fan and Modern Forms Fan Light) or the names the driver gave them. A name you typed, or a dealer typed, is never overwritten by it: both items stay as they are and the line ends ; names kept.
  • Name Items After Fan is the way back. It is an action on the Actions tab, a click and never programmed. It names both items after the fan whatever they are called now, and says what it did. It works from the model the driver remembers, so it also works while the fan is off or not answering. Clicking it again changes nothing and says so.
  • Two fans of one model get the same name. The driver adds no number and no room. Rename one of the items yourself: the driver then leaves it alone.
  • A model the driver has no name for gets no name: Fan Model keeps the model number (Gen 3, fan 9999-5-120, light kit F4IN-120V-R1-30, Breeze) and nothing is renamed. Note the Fan Model text and open an issue on the project's GitHub page (chapter 18); a later driver can add the name.
  • The name never comes from the Modern Forms app. The fan's name in the app is often a room name, and the driver does not even keep it (chapter 17).
  • After a rename choose File » Refresh Navigators so the touch screens and the app show the new names. A voice assistant that knows the fan by its old name needs a new discovery, and a routine that says the old name needs the new one. Anything that looks the fan up by its name, such as some third-party drivers, will not find it under the old name.

Updating the driver

A new release is installed the same way: Driver » Add or Update Driver or Agent…, both files at once. Composer updates every device of the two drivers in place, so the fan item, its light, the Breeze fan, their bindings, programming and settings all stay; Driver Version then shows the new build. Never delete the device and add it again to update it (chapter 12). Rows that a release adds come with their defaults, and the keypad LED animations stay off until you turn them on. Most updates need nothing more and no controller reboot. Two need a step of their own:

  • From release 1.2 with a Breeze switch: the switch is replaced by the Breeze fan (Upgrading from 1.2, chapter 9).
  • From release 1.4 or earlier: one controller reboot, for the light's fades (below).
An update from release 1.4 or earlier needs one controller reboot. Release 1.5 made the light fade, and the light's Click Rates rows (Ramp Up and Ramp Down) are part of what Control4 reads only when it first loads a driver. Composer asks Restart Director?; answering Yes does not load them. Reboot the controller from System Manager instead. Until you do, the light works, but its Click Rates rows may be missing or go back to 0.250 and 0.750 seconds after you set them, and a tap may then change the light at once instead of fading. After the reboot the rows keep what you set: check that Ramp Up and Ramp Down read 3.000 seconds, or the time you want (Fades, chapter 6). If a device's light settings page ever shows no Click Rates rows, the same reboot brings them.

4. Moving from the stock WAC driver

A Control4 device cannot be switched to another driver, and this driver never takes over from the stock one (wac_mf_fan_and_light_controller(v4).c4z) by itself. The move is done by hand in Composer; no action of the driver does it or reports on it. Back up the project first. The stock device stays until the last step, so every step before that can be undone.

  1. Add this driver's device beside the stock one, in the fan's room (chapter 3). While both exist, give the two new items names you can tell apart, for example "Fan (new)" and "Fan Light (new)". The driver keeps names you typed; do not click Name Items After Fan unless you want the fan's product name instead.
  2. Find and use the fan: pick the fan in Found Fans, which the driver fills by itself, and click Set; or type its address in Fan Address. Go on once Status reads Online: ….
  3. Move each keypad button. First take the button away from the stock device: disable the programming lines that send its presses to the stock fan or light, or disconnect it from a stock link it is bound to. Then bind it to this driver's link (chapter 7), so that a press never reaches the fan through both drivers. Disable the programming that lights or darkens the button's LED as well: it names only the keypad, so deleting the stock device would not remove it, and it fights the link. A double tap stays programming: add a line on the button's own Double Tap event, aimed at the new fan, and set Speed N Link Also Lit At so that the button stays lit at that speed (chapter 7).
  4. Move every other line of programming that commands or tests the stock fan or its light, in scripts, timers, scheduler events and macros: aim it at the new fan or light, or disable it. A script on one of the stock items' own events (such as one that runs when its speed changes) is disabled, and rebuilt on the new item's event if it is still needed. In Programming, go through the events of the stock device's two items, of the keypads and of the agents. Outside programming, check Advanced Lighting Scenes, Navigator favourites and voice assistants' routines that name the fan.
  5. Check it. Press each moved button and watch the fan and the LED, and use the fan and the light on a Navigator. Until the next step, a disabled line can be enabled again and a binding disconnected.
  6. Remove the old setup. Delete the old lines you disabled (the LED lines too), then the stock device. Rename the new items to the names the family knows, choose File » Refresh Navigators and back up the project again. Do not keep both devices longer than needed: two drivers asking one fan can make it slow or freeze it (chapter 2).

Never delete and re-add this driver's device once buttons are bound to it (chapter 12). One house's move, step by step and with a way back from each step, is recorded on GitHub.

5. The fan

Speeds and Navigators

The fan is a Control4 fan with six speeds, shown as Level 1 to Level 6 (the names the stock driver used, so Navigators and programming that say "Level 2" look the same after the move), and off. A speed chosen on a Navigator, in programming or by a keypad link is sent at once as one request that turns the fan on at that speed; off is one request that turns it off. Control4's own commands are always sent, even when they equal what the driver last heard, because what it last heard can be up to one poll old. A fast series of taps ends on the last one: the requests are queued one at a time with a quarter second between them, and the newest wish wins.

On, Off, Toggle and the preset speed

  • On and Toggle (from off) turn the fan on at the preset speed, Level 3 until you change it. On a fan that already runs, On changes nothing, so a stray On never changes a speed you chose.
  • Off and Toggle (from on) turn the fan off.
  • The preset is the fan item's Preset Level in Composer (Control4's Designate Preset). The driver remembers it through updates, restarts and reboots.
  • On a fan with Breeze the fan item shows fan mode only: while Breeze blows it counts as off. On and Toggle then switch the fan from Breeze to fan mode at the preset speed (the blades keep turning), and Off stops the fan. Every start from the fan item is fan mode, and every Off from it clears Breeze (Breeze, chapter 8).

Cycling

The fan proxy's own Cycle Speed Up and Cycle Speed Down step from the shown speed and stop at Level 6 and at Level 1; they never turn the fan off. The driver's Fan Cycle link for keypads works the same way upward: from off it goes to Level 1, then one speed up per press, and at Level 6 it stays at Level 6. It never turns the fan off; bind a second button to Fan Off for that. While Breeze blows the fan item counts as off: Cycle Speed Up and the Fan Cycle link go to Level 1 in fan mode, which ends Breeze, and Cycle Speed Down does nothing.

Direction

The fan can run forward or in reverse. A direction chosen on a Navigator or in programming (set, or toggle) is written to the fan as its own request and shown in Control4's fan state; Status adds reverse while the fan runs in reverse. A change made with the remote shows up at the next poll.

Changes made elsewhere

The remote, the wall control and the Modern Forms app can change the fan at any time. The driver notices at the next poll (every 15 seconds, or sooner: after a change it asks every 2 seconds for 20 seconds), and then behaves as if Control4 had done it: the Navigator tile moves, the fan's events fire, the keypad LEDs follow, and Last Change By says who did it (chapter 13). One notification is sent per real change, and never an older one after a newer one.

What programming gets

  • The fan's own events, conditionals and variables from Control4's fan proxy appear under the fan item in Programming with Control4's wording: the fan turning on, turning off, its speed changing, its direction changing, whether it is on, off, at a given speed, or reversed. On a fan with Breeze they follow fan mode: when Breeze starts, the fan item turns off and its speed changes to 0, though the blades keep turning, and when Breeze ends in fan mode it turns on again (Breeze and the fan item's own events, chapter 8).
  • The driver adds four events, Fan Offline, Fan Online, Breeze Started and Breeze Stopped; two variables, Online and Breeze; two conditions, Fan is online and Breeze is on; and four commands for Breeze (Breeze, chapter 8, and chapter 14). Fan Offline also fires when the wrong fan, a Gen 4 fan or a device that is not a fan answers at the address. A fan that the driver follows to a new address shows as one Fan Offline and one Fan Online; the move has no event of its own (chapter 12).
  • The light's events (on, off, level changed) are the light's own, in Programming under the light item.

6. The light

The light is an ordinary Control4 dimmer with on, off and a level from 1 to 100 %. The fan keeps its light's level while the light is off. There is no colour and no colour temperature. Its settings page (select the light item) is Control4's own, and it fades, within the limits below. One more setting of the light is on the fan item's Properties tab: Light Min On (%), for a light kit that stays dark at its lowest levels (Light Min On, below).

Turning on and the Default On Brightness

A tap on a Top button, a tap on a Toggle button while the light is off, Control4's On command and a Navigator's on all turn the light on to the Default On Brightness, 100 % until you change it on the light item's settings page (1 to 100). A level chosen with the slider or in programming is exactly that level. To turn the light on at a level other than the one the fan kept, the driver first sets the level and then switches the light on, so the light never flashes at its old level; that is two requests, one after the other. A fade from off does the same with its first small level: it switches the light on at a low level (about 0.4 seconds' worth of the fade) and fades up from there.

Fades

The fan's light cannot fade by itself: it takes a level and goes to it. The driver fades it by stepping the level, and the times come from Control4's own settings for a light, on the light item's settings page (Composer shows rates in seconds, 3.000, and takes a new value when you press Enter).

  • A tap fades. The light's Click Rates (Ramp Up and Ramp Down) are the time for the whole range, 3 seconds each unless you change them, so a shorter way takes its share: a tap from 50 % to 100 % takes 1.5 seconds, and a tap from off to 100 % takes 3. Top (and Toggle while the light is off) fades up to the Default On Brightness, or down to it from above; Bottom (and Toggle while the light is on) fades off. A tap that finds the light already at the Default On Brightness changes nothing.
  • A second tap stops the fade. A tap on any bound light button while the light fades stops it where it is, as a tap does on a Control4 dimmer, and the click that follows does nothing. Two presses of one button within about 0.6 seconds are a double tap, left to the keypad's own Double Tap event: the second one neither starts nor stops anything. The light stops at the last level the fan confirmed, which can be one step after the press. Control4's Stop commands for a brightness ramp and for a scene ramp stop it the same way.
  • Commands without a time (Control4's On, Off and Toggle, a Navigator's on and off, Set Level, and a ramp or target in programming that gives no time) use the Default Brightness Rate of the light's settings page. A new light is normally set to 0.75 seconds, which is too short to fade (below), so these commands then act at once; set the rate to 3 seconds, or any rate of a second or more, and they fade. The driver keeps the rate Control4 sends it; until Control4 has sent one it assumes 0.75 seconds.
  • Commands with a time (Ramp To Level with a time, Set Brightness Target with a rate) fade over exactly that time, for that one change. A time of 0 is instant.
  • A hold works as on a dimmer: a press held longer than 0.4 seconds brightens (Top, or Toggle while the light is off) toward 100 % or dims (Bottom, or Toggle while it is on) toward 1 %. The whole range takes 5 seconds unless you change the Hold Ramp Rates on the settings page, at that speed from any level, and it stops where you let go. It moves in small steps, by time, as a fade does. Held while the light is off, Top or Toggle turns it on at 1 % and brightens from there (about 0.6 seconds later). Bottom does nothing while the light is off. A tap that arrives within a second of letting go of a hold on the same button is ignored, because some keypads send one. A hold that starts while a fade runs stops the fade and goes on from where the light stopped.
  • The Navigator's slider jumps. Control4 sends a slider's levels with no time, so each is sent at once; while one request is on its way only the newest level is kept, so the light follows the slider in a few jumps and ends where the slider ends.
  • To turn fades off, set the Click Rates to 0 (taps act at once) and use a time of 0 in programming; there is no separate setting.

What a fade looks like, and its limits

  • A staircase, not a smooth ramp. The fan takes one request at a time, a few a second, so a 3-second fade is about 7 to 18 steps, bigger when the fan answers slowly. The driver plans the line from the old level to the new one by the clock and sends the level it should be at now, so a slow answer makes the next step bigger and the fade still ends on time, give or take a few tenths of a second. It cannot be smoother than the fan allows.
  • Too short or too small to fade. A fade of under about 1 second, or over fewer than 3 levels, is sent as one request, as is a rate of 0. That includes the Default Brightness Rate at 0.75 seconds.
  • A new command replaces the fade. A tap that is not a stop, an On, an Off, a scene or a slider move starts from the level the light shows now. Commands for the fan itself (speed, direction, Breeze) do not interrupt a fade; the next step is a little bigger.
  • Changes made outside Control4. The Modern Forms app, the remote and the wall control do not know about the fade. A level set in the app during a short fade may be overwritten by the driver's next step. Turning the light off from outside (or the fan's own timer doing it) ends the fade at once and is never undone: the driver does not turn the light back on. During a long fade, a read of the fan that shows a level the fade did not set ends the fade too, and the change is reported as made outside Control4.
  • After a fade to off the driver writes the level the light started from back into the fan, while the light is dark, so that the Modern Forms app, the remote or the wall control turn the light on where it was and not at 1 %. This is one more request; nothing is written if the fan keeps that level already.
  • A failed step ends the fade. If a step fails or is given up, the fade stops where the light is, one warning goes to the log, and Control4 is told the real level. When the fan answers again the driver sends the fade's target once, as one jump; it is never sent after the fan has gone offline, and nothing is retried step by step.
  • Nothing resumes. A fade is not stored. After a driver restart, a controller reboot or an update during a fade, the light stays where its last step left it and the first contact tells Control4 that level; the fade is not finished.
  • Fades are paced. Many fades in a row are held back a little, to keep the fan's small web server from being flooded, so a fade started right after several others can look coarser. The final level of every fade is always sent.
  • A rate changed during a fade counts from the next fade.
  • After an update from release 1.4 or earlier the light's Click Rates take effect only after one controller reboot (Updating the driver, chapter 3); until then a tap may change the light at once.

Light Min On: a light that stays dark at its lowest levels

Many LED light kits give no light at all at the bottom of their range: the fan accepts the level, but nothing glows until a level some way up. A light dimmed up from off then seems to start late, and the lowest part of the slider does nothing you can see. A Control4 dimmer has a setting for this, Min On, and Light Min On (%) is the same thing for the fan's light. The fan has no such setting of its own, so the driver does the work: it moves Control4's 1 % up to the fan level you give, keeps Control4's 100 % at the fan's 100, and spreads the levels in between evenly over what is left. With Light Min On (%) at 20, Control4's 1 % is the fan's level 20, its 40 % the fan's 52 and its 50 % the fan's 60. Control4 still sees a light from 1 to 100 %, and its 1 % is now the first level that lights.

  • Where it is. A row on the fan item's Properties tab, below Poll Interval (s): 0 to 99, a whole number; type the value and click Set. It is not under Range Levels on the light item's settings page, where a Control4 dimmer has its Min On, because Control4 has deprecated Range Levels for drivers like this one, so a driver setting is the way left. There is no Max On.
  • 0, the default, changes nothing. At 0, and at 1, every level is the fan's own, as before. Whatever the value, 0 % is still off, and a light that is on never shows 0 %.
  • Finding the value. With Light Min On (%) at 0, so that Control4's levels and the fan's are the same numbers, turn the light off and bring it up one level at a time: 1 %, then 2 %, 3 % and so on, from a Navigator's slider or with Set Level in programming (the Modern Forms app shows the same numbers then and works too). Note the first level at which the light visibly glows, and set Light Min On (%) a few points above it, as Control4 suggests for the Min On of its own dimmers: a light kit that first glows at 12 gets about 15. Then try it: the slider at 1 % should glow, and a tap or a hold from off should light at once. Keep the value as low as gives a clean start.
  • Changing it. With the light off nothing is sent; the next turn-on uses the new value. With the light on at a level Control4 set, the light keeps its Control4 level and moves once, at once and without a fade, to the fan level that now goes with it. With the light on at a level set in the Modern Forms app, the remote or the wall control, the light is not touched and Control4's level changes instead. A fade, hold or scene ramp that is running finishes with the old value, and the new one applies when it ends. A value that is not a number, below 0, or 100 or more is refused: the row goes back to the old value and the log says so. A decimal is rounded to a whole number. Each change writes one line to the log, for example Light Min On 20: Control4's 1 % is the fan's level 20.
  • Times stay the same. The Click Rates, the Hold Ramp Rates, the Default Brightness Rate and the times given in programming still count over Control4's whole range: a tap from off to 100 % still takes 3 seconds at the default rate, and a hold over the whole range 5 seconds. They now cover fewer of the fan's levels, all of them lit, so no part of the time is spent dark. A fade from off is lit from its first step, a hold from off starts at 1 %, which now glows, and a hold down stops at 1 %, which still glows.
  • Levels set outside Control4. The Modern Forms app, the remote and the wall control still set any level of the fan. A level below the Min On shows in Control4 as 1 % (the light is on), and the driver never raises it or changes it. Status shows both numbers while the value is above 1: Online: fan speed 3, light 40 % (fan 52), or Online: fan off, light 1 % (fan 5, below Min On) for a level below it (Status texts, chapter 14).
  • The Modern Forms app's numbers no longer match Control4's. The app, the remote and the wall control show the fan's own levels, so at 20 the app reads 52 % while Control4 shows 40 %. Navigators, keypads, scenes, programming and the Default On Brightness all use Control4's levels. A Default On Brightness below the Min On's number is not a mistake: it is a Control4 level too, and lands above the Min On on the fan.
  • Check it again after the fan is replaced (when Use Selected Fan takes another fan with a Min On set, the log says Light Min On is 20 %; check it for this fan's light), after a new light kit, and after a firmware update of the fan: each can change where the light starts to glow. The driver never resets or guesses the value. It belongs to the Control4 device, and a restored project brings it back with the other properties.
  • High values leave few levels. The higher the value, the fewer of the fan's levels Control4's 100 steps share: at 50 there are 51, and near 99 only two are left (at 99, Control4's 1 to 50 % is the fan's 99 and 51 to 100 % its 100), so the light becomes little more than on and off. A value well above the first glowing level gives nothing back.
  • The light only. It changes the levels of the fan's light and nothing else: not the fan's speeds, not the keypad LEDs of the light's links (they show on and off, never a level), and it has nothing to do with Breeze LED Night Brightness (%), which dims the Breeze animation on the keypad at night (chapter 10). On a fan without a light kit it is kept and does nothing.

Advanced Lighting Scenes

The light can be a member of Control4's Advanced Lighting Scenes. A scene's level is set over the scene's time (at once, if that time is 0 or short, as above). Ramp-up and ramp-down commands of a scene start a ramp at the hold rate, toward 100 % or 1 %, and the stop commands stop it where it is; these have not been tried on a real scene yet.

No light kit, and a fan that is away

A fan without a light kit shows no light kit in Status and Fan Model. The light item then stays unavailable and its commands are refused and logged. While the fan is offline, the light's controls are greyed out on Navigators; coming back, they return.

Binding a button

  1. In Composer open Connections and select the keypad (or the Switch V2 or Essential Switch that is set to Use as 2 Button Keypad). The top pane lists its buttons as <button> Button Link inputs; the lower list BUTTON_LINK Output Devices lists the Modern Forms Fan's links under the fan item.
  2. Drag the button's row from the top pane onto the link's row in the lower list. (A drag that starts in the lower list does nothing.)
  3. Read both lists again: each shows the binding. The fan's Keypad Links row now counts it and names the keypad. The first button on a Fan Speed link also brings up the keypad LED rows (Keypad LED settings, below).
  4. To undo it, right-click the binding and choose Disconnect; the prompt names the two devices, not the link, so check the lists again afterwards.
Follow Connection. Making the connection switches the button's LED Behavior to Follow Connection by itself, so the LED follows the link. Disconnecting does not switch it back: set Programmed again yourself if the button's LED should obey programming once more. A button that still has programming that lights or darkens its LED ("Use the on color for…") will fight the link, because both paint the same LED: remove that programming when you bind the button (chapter 4). The driver never changes a keypad's settings.

What a press does

  • A fan link, Fan Breeze included, acts at once on the press. A keypad that only sends clicks is handled too: a click acts when no press arrived on that link in the previous 10 seconds. Letting go does nothing, and there is no hold and no repeat on the fan links: holding Fan Speed 3 sets Level 3 once.
  • A double tap is two presses and acts twice. For a button whose double tap should do something else, use the keypad's own Double Tap event in programming (and its LED rule, Speed N Link Also Lit At, below): the fan may briefly go to the single-tap speed first, and the Double Tap line then sets the other speed. That relies on your keypad delivering its Double Tap event for a bound button; check it on yours. A double tap on a button bound to Fan Breeze toggles twice: from a stopped fan it starts Breeze and stops the fan again, and from fan mode the first press starts Breeze and the second stops the fan. It no longer ends where it started, as it did in release 1.2.
  • The light links (Top, Bottom, Toggle) act on a press as Control4's own dimmer keypad does: a tap starts a fade, a hold dims or brightens while it is held, and a press on one of them while the light fades stops the fade at once. Two presses of one button within about 0.6 seconds are a double tap and are left to the keypad's own Double Tap event: the second one neither starts nor stops anything (chapter 6).
  • Several keypads may be bound to the same link; they share it.
  • Fan offline: a press is tried once, as the check whether the fan is there, and dropped if it fails; it is never replayed. Wrong fan, Gen 4, not a fan, no address, rebooting: the press is refused and the LED stays as it was.

Up and Down buttons

A keypad's Up and Down buttons are often Regular Buttons without an LED, but they have Button Link inputs too. Bind Up to Light Top and Down to Light Bottom: a tap fades the light to the Default On Brightness or off over the light's click rates, a second tap stops the fade, and a hold brightens (Up) or dims (Down) it (chapter 6). These buttons have no LED to follow.

The LEDs

  • When you press a bound button its LED shows the target at once; when the fan answers otherwise, or the request fails, the LEDs are corrected. A change made with the remote, the app or the wall control moves the LEDs at the next poll.
  • Right after the driver starts, while the fan's state is not known yet, no LED is touched (a newly bound button is told "off"). While the fan is offline the LEDs keep their last state.
  • In Breeze mode every fan link is dark except Fan Breeze: the speed, Toggle and Cycle links follow fan mode, and Fan Off lights only while the fan is stopped. A keypad with no button on Fan Breeze therefore shows every fan LED dark while the fan blows a breeze; bind a button to Fan Breeze if one should light. With Per speed colours the dark links take the off colour, as for a stopped fan. With Breeze LED Animation on Gusts, the speed buttons play gusts instead (The Breeze animation on the speed buttons, chapter 10).
  • At a fixed speed, with Speed LED Animation on, the lit speed button goes dark for a moment every rest while a dot in its colour runs across the speed buttons (The speed chase at a fixed speed, chapter 10).
  • In a dark room, once a keypad's ambient light reading is connected to the fan, both animations pause and send nothing while that keypad is dark (Pausing the animations in the dark, chapter 10).
  • Colours. By default the driver sends no colours on the fan links: a bound fan button shows the on and off colours set on the keypad itself. A button bound to Fan Breeze always shows the keypad's own colours. Set Speed LED Colors to Per speed for a colour of its own at each speed (below). The light links tell the keypad the colours of the light item's settings page (blue by default; Top and Toggle lit while the light is on, Bottom lit while it is off). While the light fades, they go by where the light is heading: a tap that starts a fade off darkens Top and Toggle and lights Bottom at once, and a tap that starts a fade on does the reverse, instead of waiting for the end of the fade.

Keypad LED settings

Twenty-one rows on the fan item's Properties tab shape what the buttons on the speed links show: the six Speed N Link Also Lit At rows, the colour rows (Speed LED Colors, Speed 1 LED Color to Speed 6 LED Color, Speed LED Off Color) and the animation rows (the four Breeze LED rows, Speed LED Animation, Speed LED Rest (s) and Pause LED Animations Below). They act only on buttons bound to Fan Speed 1 to Fan Speed 6, so Composer shows them only while at least one button is bound to one of those six links. Keypad Links is always shown, and its first item says which case you are in.

Without a keypad (no button on a Fan Speed link):

  • The rows are hidden, and there is nothing to do. The fan, the light and the other links work as described in this chapter, and buttons on the other links follow the fan in the keypad's own colours. A device shows the rows for about ten seconds after it is added, and after the driver starts or is updated, while the driver reads its links; then they go.
  • Keypad Links reads, for example, 0 of 13 links bound; LED settings hidden: bind a button to a Fan Speed link.
  • Only the speed links count. Fan Off, Fan Toggle, Fan Cycle, Fan Breeze and the light's links never take the driver's colours or animations, so a keypad bound only to them, such as a 2-button keypad on Fan Toggle and Fan Off, leaves the rows hidden. The light's LED colours are on the light item's own settings page, which is always shown.
  • To make them appear, bind a keypad button to one of the Fan Speed links in Connections (Binding a button, above). They appear at once. If the Properties tab was open and has not changed, select the fan item again.
  • Values are kept. Hiding a row never changes it: a value set earlier stays, is saved with the project, and is there again when the rows come back.

With a keypad (a button on at least one Fan Speed link):

  • The rows appear at once when the first button is bound to a Fan Speed link, with the values they had. Keypad Links then reads, for example, 4 of 13 links bound; LED settings shown.
  • Any device on a Fan Speed link counts. Control4 does not tell the driver what kind of device is bound, so it cannot tell a keypad with colour LEDs from another device on a speed link: the rows are shown for either.
  • What the rows do, each group with its own section:
    • Speed 1 Link Also Lit At to Speed 6 Link Also Lit At: a second speed at which a speed button is lit (Speed N Link Also Lit At).
    • Speed LED Colors, Speed 1 LED Color to Speed 6 LED Color and Speed LED Off Color: a colour of its own for each speed (Speed LED Colors).
    • Breeze LED Animation, Breeze LED Step (ms) and Breeze LED Night Brightness (%): gusts on the speed buttons while the fan blows a breeze (The Breeze animation, chapter 10).
    • Speed LED Animation and Speed LED Rest (s): a chase across the speed buttons at a fixed speed (The speed chase, chapter 10).
    • Breeze LED Bottom: which end of the speed buttons is at the bottom of the keypad, for both animations (chapter 10).
    • Pause LED Animations Below: the keypad reading below which both animations pause (Pausing the animations in the dark, chapter 10).
  • A fan without Breeze. On a fan that reports no Breeze (its Fan Model does not end in , Breeze), Breeze LED Animation, Breeze LED Step (ms) and Breeze LED Night Brightness (%) stay hidden even with a keypad. Breeze LED Bottom stays, because the speed chase uses it too. Until the driver knows whether the fan has Breeze, the three rows are shown.
  • The last speed button removed. When no button is left on a Fan Speed link, the rows go about 8 seconds later; the wait keeps them from flickering while a button is moved from one link to another. Their values are kept. The animations have no buttons left to play on, and they carry on with the same settings when a speed button is bound again.

Speed N Link Also Lit At

A button normally lights at its own speed only. Some houses give a button a second job through programming: a single tap sets one speed and a double tap, on the keypad's own Double Tap event, sets another. Without help that button would go dark at the second speed. Each speed link therefore has a row on the fan item (shown while a button is bound to a Fan Speed link, above), Speed 1 Link Also Lit At to Speed 6 Link Also Lit At, with the choices No other speed (the default) and Speed 1 to Speed 6. The link's LED is then lit while the fan runs at its own speed or at that one.

Example. A button is bound to Fan Speed 2 and its Double Tap event sets Level 1. Set Speed 2 Link Also Lit At to Speed 1 and click Set: the button is lit at Level 2 and at Level 1. This is an LED rule only; the double tap itself stays keypad programming. Choosing a link's own speed means the same as No other speed. A change of the row is applied to the LED at once.

Speed LED Colors: a colour per speed

A button on Follow Connection has one on colour, the one set on the keypad. To show a different colour at each speed (for example a button lit blue at Level 1 and orange at Level 6), set Speed LED Colors to Per speed and pick the colours: Speed 1 LED Color to Speed 6 LED Color are the colours a speed link shows while the fan runs at that speed, and Speed LED Off Color is the colour it shows when dark. Each is a Composer colour row: its R, G and B boxes only show the value. Click the colour swatch, choose Define Custom Colors, type Red, Green and Blue (for example 0, 0 and 255 for blue), click OK, then click Set on the row. A row already at that colour shows no Set. The rows appear only once a button is bound to a Fan Speed link (Keypad LED settings, above), so bind the buttons first, then set the colours, and switch Speed LED Colors to Per speed last, so the buttons never show the default blue in between. With Keypad's own (the default) the driver sends no colours and the six colour rows do nothing.

The driver sends the colours over the link, just before it lights the button, and again whenever you change a colour while the button is lit. A button that is bound later, or a keypad that asks, gets them too. The Off, Toggle, Cycle and Breeze links and the light's links are not affected.

Back to the keypad's own colours. Set Speed LED Colors to Keypad's own and click Set: the driver sends no more colours, and both LED animations stop, since they need Per speed (chapter 10). Nothing is sent to undo the colours already sent: Control4 gives the driver no way to read or restore a keypad's own colours, so the keypad keeps the last colours it was sent until its own button colours are set again on the keypad's settings in Composer. To stop only an animation, use its own row (Turning the animations off, chapter 10): that is the clean way off.

The Keypad Links row

Keypad Links lists the bindings: the first item reads 5 of 13 links bound; LED settings shown, then one item per binding, for example Fan Speed 3: Example Keypad (456) or Fan Breeze: Example Keypad (456) (the link without "Button Link", the keypad's name and its device id). The first item ends in ; LED settings shown while a button is bound to a Fan Speed link, and in ; LED settings hidden: bind a button to a Fan Speed link otherwise (Keypad LED settings, above). The row is never hidden. Picking an item changes nothing.

8. Breeze

Some Gen 3 fans have Breeze: the fan varies its speed by itself, in gusts, like a natural breeze, at a strength of 1, 2 or 3. Fan Model ends in , Breeze when the fan has it. On such a fan Control4 can switch Breeze; on any other fan nothing about Breeze is sent (below).

Fan mode or Breeze mode, never both

The fan's motor is always in one of three states: stopped; fan mode, running at one of its six speeds; or Breeze mode, blowing a breeze at strength 1, 2 or 3, where the six fan speeds mean nothing. Control4 shows the two modes on two devices:

  • The fan item shows fan mode only. In Breeze mode its tile shows off (speed 0), though the blades turn; its speed, Toggle and Cycle LEDs are dark (unless the speed buttons play the Breeze animation), and so is the Fan Off LED, because Off would stop the fan (chapter 7).
  • The Breeze fan (chapter 9) shows Breeze mode only: Breeze 1, 2 or 3 while Breeze blows, off otherwise.
  • A stopped fan is off on both.

Starting one mode ends the other: a speed on the fan item ends Breeze, a Breeze speed on the Breeze fan ends fan mode, and the blades keep turning in between.

"Breeze is on" means Breeze mode. A stopped fan with Breeze still set counts as off, because it is not blowing. The variable, the events, the condition, the Fan Breeze LED and the Breeze fan all follow that one meaning. Status shows the rest: Breeze 2 in Breeze mode, in place of the speed (the number is the strength), and fan off Breeze set while the fan is stopped with Breeze still set. A new strength changes Status and the Breeze fan's tile; the fan item fires no event and sets no variable for it.

Breeze and the fan item's own events

The fan item's events, conditionals and variables from Control4's fan proxy (the fan turning off and on, its speed changing, whether it is off, its current speed) follow fan mode: when Breeze starts, the fan item turns off and its speed changes to 0; when Breeze ends in fan mode, it turns on at the speed the fan reports. Programming on those events therefore also runs when Breeze starts or ends. Changes made elsewhere

Breeze switched with the Modern Forms app, or with a remote or wall control that has Breeze, shows up at the next poll like any other change: the fan item, the Breeze fan, the variable, the events, the keypad LEDs and Status follow, and Last Change By says who did it. The driver never switches it back. The Modern Forms app shows the fan running while the fan item shows off in Breeze mode: that is intended. The remote's power button during Breeze stops the fan but may keep Breeze set (Status reads fan off Breeze set); the remote's next power-on then resumes Breeze, and only the Breeze fan comes on. An Off from Control4, on either device, clears a Breeze that is still set.

The fan's own gusts. The fan may report its gusts as speed changes. They reach neither tile: the fan item shows off for all of Breeze, and the Breeze fan follows the strength, not the speed. While Breeze blows the driver also writes nothing to Last Change By for a speed change the fan reports by itself.

Variable, events and condition

Their texts in Programming are in chapter 14.

  • The variable Breeze is 1 in Breeze mode, 0 otherwise. It is read-only: switch Breeze with the commands. It is remembered across restarts and updates, so a restart alone fires no event; if Breeze changed while the driver was not running, the fan's first answer fires the event once. While the fan is offline the variable keeps its last value.
  • The events Breeze Started and Breeze Stopped fire once per change, whoever made it: Control4, the app, the remote, or the fan coming up in Breeze when it starts.
  • The condition Breeze is on, True or False, for If and While lines.
No loops in programming. A line on Breeze Started that sends Breeze Off, together with a line on Breeze Stopped that sends Breeze On, switches Breeze on and off for as long as both lines exist. So does programming that turns one of the two devices on when the other turns off, for example "when the Breeze fan turns off, set the fan to Level 2" together with "when the fan turns off, set the Breeze fan to Breeze 1": the fan goes back and forth between the two modes. The driver has no guard against it: each event fires once the fan has confirmed the change, so the loop runs at the fan's pace, one request at a time, until you remove one of the lines.

A fan without Breeze, and a fan that is away

A fan without Breeze (Gen 1 and 2 fans, and Gen 3 fans without the hardware: their Fan Model has no , Breeze): nothing about Breeze is ever sent to the fan, and the fan item works as chapter 5 describes. The commands and a press on the Fan Breeze link are refused and Last Action says so; the variable stays 0, the condition is False, no event fires, and Add Breeze Fan adds nothing. The commands stay listed in Programming, because the driver is the same for every fan.

The fan away. While the fan is offline a Breeze command is tried once and dropped if it fails, as every command is (chapter 11). With no fan set up, another fan, a Gen 4 fan or a device that is not a fan at the address, or the fan rebooting, it is refused and Last Action says why.

9. The Breeze fan for the Navigators

Control4's fan has no place for a mode such as Breeze, so the fan item cannot show it. The Navigators get a second fan instead: the Breeze fan, from a second driver, modernforms_breeze_fan.c4z (Modern Forms Breeze Fan). It is a Control4 fan with three speeds, Breeze 1, Breeze 2 and Breeze 3, and off; it has no direction and no light. The Navigators (the Control4 app, touch screens and the others) list it with the room's fans, and it works in programming like any fan.

  1. Install both packages, modernforms_fan.c4z and modernforms_breeze_fan.c4z, through Driver » Add or Update Driver or Agent… (chapter 3).   2. Select the fan item, open its Actions tab and click Add Breeze Fan.
  3. The driver adds one Breeze fan to the fan item's room, named after the fan item followed by Breeze (for example Aviator 5 Breeze, at most 30 characters), and connects it to the fan's Breeze Switch output. Last Action reads Breeze fan added: Aviator 5 Breeze.
  4. Put it right after the fan. A new device does not go next to the fan on the Navigators by itself: where Composer lets you set the order in which the room's devices are listed, move Aviator 5 Breeze directly after Aviator 5.
  5. Choose File » Refresh Navigators. The touch screens and the app show the Breeze fan only after that; check the order on one of them.

Upgrading from 1.2: the Breeze switch becomes the Breeze fan

Release 1.2 showed Breeze on the Navigators with the Breeze switch, an on/off light from the driver Modern Forms Breeze (modernforms_breeze.c4z).   3. Delete the old Breeze switch (for example Aviator 5 Breeze, driver Modern Forms Breeze) in Composer.
  4. Click Add Breeze Fan on the fan item (above). Last Action reads Breeze fan added: Aviator 5 Breeze.

10. The keypad LED animations

Two animations can play on the LEDs of the keypad buttons bound to the fan's speed links: gusts while the fan blows a breeze, and a short chase in the speed's colour at a fixed speed. The driver plays them over its own links. Both are off until you turn them on, and both need Speed LED Colors set to Per speed (Speed LED Colors, chapter 7). Their rows are on the fan item's Properties tab once a button is bound to a Fan Speed link (Keypad LED settings, chapter 7). No programming is needed and none is offered: they bring no command, event or variable. With a keypad's ambient light reading connected, both pause while that keypad is dark (Pausing the animations in the dark, below).

Which buttons take part, and in what order

Only buttons bound to Fan Speed 1 to Fan Speed 6 take part, one place in the column for each speed link that has a button: a keypad with buttons on Fan Speed 2 to Fan Speed 5 plays over four buttons, and a speed with no button leaves no gap. Every button on one link shows the same colour at the same moment, so several keypads bound to the same links play in step. Fan Off, Fan Toggle, Fan Cycle, Fan Breeze and the light's three links never take part and follow the fan as before (in Breeze mode Off, Toggle and Cycle dark, Fan Breeze lit). They show the keypad's own colours, which the driver cannot read: a colour of an animation sent to Off would stay on it as its colour afterwards, and Off would light in it the next time the fan stops. A keypad with no button on a speed link, for example a 2-button keypad, or a switch used as one, bound to Fan Toggle and Fan Off, shows no animation: its LEDs work as before.

The order, and Breeze LED Bottom. The driver cannot see where a button sits on the keypad: Control4 tells it which links are bound, not where the buttons are. It orders them by speed number, the lowest speed at the bottom, which suits a keypad with the highest speed at the top. On a keypad laid out the other way round, with the lowest speed at the top, set Breeze LED Bottom to Highest speed. Despite its name, the row orders the buttons for both animations. It belongs to the fan device, so all its keypads share it: on a second keypad laid out the other way round from the first, the animations run against the airflow.

The Breeze animation on the speed buttons

While the fan blows a breeze, the keypad buttons bound to its speed links can show it on their LEDs: every few seconds a gust, a white light trailed by a twinkling tail of ice, teal and sky blue, runs along the dark buttons the way the fan blows. It is off until you turn it on (below).

What it looks like, for example on a keypad with buttons on Fan Speed 2 to Fan Speed 5:

  • Breeze starts: every speed button goes dark at once, whatever it showed, and the first gust follows at once. The Fan Breeze button lights as usual.
  • Breeze blows: between gusts every speed button is dark. A gust is a front that runs along the speed buttons the way the fan blows: down from the top button to the bottom one while the fan blows down (forward, the summer setting), up from the bottom while it blows up (reverse), one button per step. The button it is on lights white, and the buttons just behind it form the gust's tail, which travels with it: each one twinkles, blinking off and on at its own random pace and relighting in ice, teal or sky blue, and the tail's end goes dark as the head moves on. When the head passes the last button it carries on out of sight and the tail follows it off the column, so a tail never lingers behind a head that has gone and a twinkle never runs backwards. A button ahead of the front may glint once. A change of the fan's direction during Breeze turns the next gust. Every gust is drawn at random, so no two look alike, and sometimes a second front follows close behind the first. The Breeze strength sets the weather: at Breeze 1 a gust every 4 to 8 seconds with a short tail (about half a second), at Breeze 2 every 2 to 5 seconds, at Breeze 3 every 1 to 3 seconds with the longest tail (about a second) and the most double gusts (as at Breeze 2 while the strength is not known). A new strength counts from the next gust. The fan does not report its own gusts, so the keypad's are not in time with the blades.
  • Breeze ends, whether the fan stops or goes back to fan mode: whatever is playing stops and an ending wipe plays once, at the gust's pace, the other way from the gusts: up from the bottom button after gusts that ran down, down from the top after gusts that rose. Each button the white head passes goes back to what it shows without the animation: lit in its speed's colour if the fan now runs at that speed (or at the speed of its Speed N Link Also Lit At row), dark otherwise. Then the animation is over until Breeze starts again.

The animation follows Breeze whoever starts or ends it: a keypad, a Navigator, the Breeze fan, programming, the Modern Forms app, the remote or the wall control. A change made outside Control4 shows when the driver sees it, at the next poll (chapter 5). The newest state always wins: Breeze ending during a gust starts the ending wipe at once (buttons the head has not reached yet keep what they show until it does), and Breeze coming back during an ending wipe starts the gusts again.

Turning it on. Four rows on the fan item's Properties tab, below Speed LED Off Color. They are shown once a button is bound to a Fan Speed link, and the first, third and fourth only on a fan with Breeze (Keypad LED settings, chapter 7):

Breeze LED BottomLowest speedLowest speed or Highest speed: which end of the speed buttons is at the bottom of the keypad (above). The speed chase goes by it too (below).

Blues and whites catch the eye far more than reds in a dark room, so by default the animation runs at half strength at night.   1. Bind the speed buttons to the Fan Speed links, if they are not bound yet (Binding a button, chapter 7); the rows appear then.
  2. If they are not set yet, pick the speed colours and set Speed LED Colors to Per speed (Speed LED Colors, chapter 7).
  3. Set Breeze LED Animation to Gusts and click Set. If Breeze blows at that moment, the speed buttons go dark and the first gust plays at once.
  4. Watch one gust. It should run the way the fan blows (down while the fan blows down). If it runs the other way, set Breeze LED Bottom to the other value and click Set. The row orders the speed buttons for the speed chase too, so one setting serves both.

Why Per speed. The animation recolours buttons, and when it ends it must give them back the colours they had. The driver can give back only colours it owns, the speed colours of Per speed. With Keypad's own nothing animates, and Last Action reads Breeze LEDs need Speed LED Colors set to Per speed, both when you set Gusts and when you switch Speed LED Colors away from Per speed while Gusts is set.

Which buttons take part. The speed buttons, in the order of Breeze LED Bottom (above). A fan without Breeze never animates.

A press during Breeze. A press acts as it always does (chapter 8); only its LED shows differently.

  • A speed button: the fan leaves Breeze for that speed and the ending wipe starts at once. The pressed button lights in its speed's colour as the head passes it, not at the press: with four buttons at the default step, within about half a second.
  • Fan Off or Fan Breeze: the fan stops. Off lights at once, since it is not part of the animation, Fan Breeze goes dark, and the ending wipe leaves every speed button dark.
  • A press that the fan refuses, or that fails, brings Breeze back, and with it the gusts.
  • The light's buttons change the light and nothing else; the animation goes on.

Dimming. The animation changes colours only. Every button it shows is lit, in one of four colours: white (255,255,255), ice (160,255,255), teal (0,255,255) or sky blue (64,176,255); a dark button is simply off. By day they are sent at full strength, and between sunset and sunrise scaled by Breeze LED Night Brightness (%) (above); the driver puts no other brightness into them. The keypad's own brightness for a lit LED, and its dimming by the room's light where that is on, apply on top, as to any lit LED: when the room darkens, the whole animation dims with the keypad's other LEDs. The palette leaves out pure blue (0,0,255), which a keypad dimmed for the night barely shows, so nothing vanishes when the keypad dims at night. For a brighter or dimmer animation, change the keypad's LED brightness in Composer; the driver never touches a keypad's settings. A keypad that turns its LEDs off in a dark room can also have the animation pause meanwhile (Pausing the animations in the dark, below).

Limits.

  • Button positions are unknown to the driver (above): it goes by speed number and Breeze LED Bottom.
  • The fan away. While the fan is offline, rebooting or not yet checked after a start, the animation stops at once, without a wipe, and the speed buttons show their real state (dark, as in Breeze mode). When the fan answers again and still blows a breeze, the gusts start again. Setting Breeze LED Animation to Off also stops it at once, without a wipe.
  • After a restart or an update (a driver update, a Director restart, a controller reboot) the keypad may keep the last picture of the animation until the driver's first contact with the fan, usually a few seconds after it starts. That contact gives every button its real state, and if Breeze still blows the gusts start again.
  • A button disconnected from its speed link during the animation keeps the colour it had, as any disconnected button keeps its last LED, and that may be one of the animation's colours: set its colours and its LED Behavior on the keypad (the Follow Connection note, chapter 7). A button bound to a speed link during Breeze joins the animation at once.
  • Keypad's own during Breeze. Switching Speed LED Colors to Keypad's own while the animation plays stops it at once: each speed button first gets back its Per speed colours, then the switch happens. As with any switch to Keypad's own (Speed LED Colors, chapter 7), the keypad keeps those last colours until you set its own on the keypad.
  • Programming that lights or colours the same buttons fights the animation, and the last message wins; remove it, as for any bound button.
  • The keypad network. Keypad LEDs are reached over Zigbee, the same wireless mesh that carries keypad presses and the lighting. The driver keeps the animation to at most 30 LED messages a second, all buttons together (a keypad on the author's house took 40 a second cleanly), dropping sparkles first when a gust would need more; a rest between gusts sends nothing. A shorter step looks faster but bunches the messages: raise it if the animation stutters or keypad presses and lights answer late while it plays. A step that comes late is never made up with a burst: the animation jumps to where it should be.

The speed chase at a fixed speed

While the fan runs at a fixed speed, the keypad buttons bound to its speed links can play a short chase on their LEDs every so often: the lit speed button goes dark, a single dot in that speed's colour runs across the speed buttons one after the other, the way the fan blows, and the speed button lights again. The faster the fan, the faster the dot. It is off until you turn it on (below).

What it looks like, for example on a keypad with buttons on Fan Speed 2 to Fan Speed 5, with the fan at Level 3 blowing down and the default rest:

  • At rest: the Fan Speed 3 button is lit in the Speed 3 LED Color, exactly as without the chase; so is a button lit through its Speed N Link Also Lit At row.
  • A chase: 2.5 seconds after the speed was selected the lit button goes dark, and a dot in the Speed 3 colour runs down the four buttons, from the top one to the bottom one, one button every 0.3 seconds: each button lights as the dot reaches it and goes dark as it moves on. When the dot leaves the last button, the Fan Speed 3 button lights again. While the fan blows up (reverse) the dot runs from the bottom button to the top one.
  • At the ends of the run: when the lit button is the one the dot starts on, it does not go dark first: it is the dot's first position and goes dark only as the dot moves on. When it is the one the dot ends on, the dot stays there into the rest. Either way the button never blinks twice (at Level 2 and Level 5 on a four-button keypad, depending on the direction).
  • The rest: then five seconds (the default) with the Fan Speed 3 button lit, and the next chase. The rest counts from the end of one chase to the start of the next, and it is the same at every speed: only the dot's pace changes.
  • The pace: one button every 0.4, 0.3, 0.2 and 0.12 seconds at Level 2, 3, 4 and 5, and every 0.5 seconds at Level 1 and 0.1 seconds at Level 6 when those take part. With four buttons a chase takes 1.6 seconds at Level 2 and under half a second at Level 5.
  • A new speed: the new speed's button lights at once, as it always does, and the first chase at the new speed comes 2.5 seconds later, whatever selected it: a keypad, a Navigator, programming, or the Modern Forms app, its remote or the wall control (those three once the driver sees the change at its next poll of the fan, chapter 5). After that first chase the rest applies again. Turning the chase on, starting the fan, a new direction and leaving Breeze for a speed also begin with the 2.5-second wait (after the Breeze ending wipe, if the Breeze animation is on).

The chase follows the fan whoever changes it: a keypad, a Navigator, programming, the Modern Forms app, the remote or the wall control. A change made outside Control4 shows when the driver sees it, at the next poll (chapter 5). The newest state always wins: a change of the speed, the direction, the bound speed buttons, Speed LED Animation, Speed LED Colors or Breeze LED Bottom during a chase ends it at once, every speed button goes back to what it shows without the chase, and the next chase comes 2.5 seconds later. A new Speed LED Rest (s) does not end a chase: it counts from the next rest. The dot takes the speed's colour at the start of every chase, so a new colour shows from the next one.

When it does not play. Only in fan mode, at a speed the row includes, while the fan answers, with Per speed colours, with at least two speed buttons bound, and not while a keypad connected to Ambient Light Level (Follower) is dark (Pausing the animations in the dark, below). Why Per speed. The dot is drawn in the speed's own colour, and every button it crosses must show its own colour again afterwards. The driver can do both only with colours it owns, the speed colours of Per speed. With Keypad's own nothing chases, and Last Action reads LED chase needs Speed LED Colors set to Per speed, both when you turn the chase on and when you switch Speed LED Colors away from Per speed while it is on.

Which buttons take part. The same as for the Breeze animation: the speed buttons, in the order of Breeze LED Bottom (above). A keypad with fewer than two speed buttons shows no chase. The fan's speed needs no button of its own: at a speed without one, the dot still runs over the buttons there are.

A press during a chase acts as it always does (chapter 7) and ends the chase at once: a pressed speed button lights at once, and the next chase comes 2.5 seconds later. Fan Off stops the fan, and the chase with it; Fan Breeze starts Breeze. The light's buttons change the light and nothing else; the chase goes on.

At night. The chase runs as by day, in the full speed colours: Breeze LED Night Brightness (%) does not apply to it. The keypad's own LED brightness, and its dimming by the room's light where that is on, apply to the dot as to any lit LED. The default blue (0,0,255) barely shows on a keypad dimmed for the night, so a chase in the default colours can vanish then, as the lit button does; pick lighter colours if it should show. Where a moving light is unwelcome at night, for example in a bedroom, a longer rest makes it rarer. The chase has no night setting of its own, but it can follow the keypad: with the keypad's ambient light reading connected, it pauses while the keypad is dark (Pausing the animations in the dark, below).

Where it differs from Control4's own keypads. A Control4 keypad LED shows the state of what it is bound to, and Control4 asks drivers not to keep the keypads' network busy with repeated LED messages. The chase departs from both on purpose, which is why it is off until you turn it on:

  • For the length of a chase (1.6 seconds at Level 2 on four buttons, at most 3 seconds) the speed buttons do not show the speed: the lit one is dark and the others light in turn. Between chases, and after any press, they show the fan exactly as without the feature, and every way the chase can end gives each button its real state.
  • It repeats every rest for as long as the fan runs at that speed, for hours if need be. Each chase sends about three LED messages per button, 13 for four buttons; at the default rest of 5 seconds that averages about 2 messages a second, all buttons together, and a chase stays within the 30 messages a second the Breeze animation is held to: a message that would go over waits, and none is dropped. The rest sends nothing and cannot be shorter than 5 seconds; a longer rest means less traffic, and a keypad that is dark can pause the chase altogether (Pausing the animations in the dark, below).
  • The dot steps from button to button: a button link can light, darken and colour a button, but has no fade from one to the next.
  • Programming that lights or colours the speed buttons fights the chase, and the last message wins. Keep the speed buttons following the fan's connection, without LED programming of their own (the Follow Connection note, chapter 7).

Limits.

  • Button positions are unknown to the driver: it goes by speed number and Breeze LED Bottom, one row for all the fan's keypads, so on a second keypad laid out the other way round the chase runs the other way.
  • The direction must be known: while the driver does not know which way the fan blows, there is no chase.
  • After a restart or an update (a driver update, a Director restart, a controller reboot) the keypad may keep the last picture of a chase that was playing, a dot lit or the speed button dark, until the driver's first contact with the fan, usually a few seconds after it starts. That contact gives every button its real state, and the first chase comes 2.5 seconds after it.
  • A button disconnected from its speed link during a chase keeps its last LED, as any disconnected button does (the Follow Connection note, chapter 7).
  • No controller reboot. The chase adds only property rows, so it needs no controller reboot.

Pausing the animations in the dark

A keypad dims its LEDs by the room's light, following its Ambient Light Profile, and a profile whose status LED minimum is 0 turns every status LED off in a dark room, the speed chase and the Breeze gusts included. The driver cannot see that by itself: it would go on sending the animations' messages over Zigbee, the keypad network that also carries presses and the lighting, all night, for LEDs that show nothing. Given the keypad's own ambient light reading, the driver pauses both animations while the keypad is dark, sends nothing for them, and plays them again once the room is lit. It is off until you connect a keypad.

Setting it up.

  1. Connect the reading. In Composer open Connections and select the keypad whose speed buttons play the animations. Among its outputs is Ambient Light Level (Source). Connect it to the Modern Forms Fan's input Ambient Light Level (Follower) the way you bind a button: drag the keypad's row from the top pane onto the fan's row in the lower list (Binding a button, chapter 7), then read both lists again.
  2. Let the keypad's LEDs go dark. In the keypad's Ambient Light Profile, choose Fully Customized and set the status LEDs' Min Brightness to 0, so that the keypad turns its status LEDs off in a dark room rather than only dimming them. Note the profile's Dark Room value: below that reading the keypad uses its minimum.
  3. Set the threshold. On the fan item's Properties tab, set Pause LED Animations Below to that Dark Room value and click Set. The default, 10, suits a profile whose Dark Room is 10. The animations then pause exactly while the keypad shows nothing.
  4. Check it. In the dark, Keypad Links reads, for example, 4 of 13 links bound; LED animations paused: the keypad is dark. Switch a lamp on: about 10 seconds later it reads ; LED settings shown again, and the animations come back.

How the driver decides. The keypad reports its reading, on the 0 to 255 scale of its Ambient Light Profile, whenever it changes; the driver never asks for it. The keypad counts as dark while its last reading is below Pause LED Animations Below, and the pause starts with the first such reading. It counts as lit again only once no dark reading has come for 10 seconds, so a light that flickers around the threshold (a television, a door, passing headlights) pauses the animations once and resumes them once. A new threshold applies to the last reading at once; resuming still waits the 10 seconds. At 0 no reading is below it, so the animations never pause.

What pauses, and what still updates.

  • The speed chase: no chase plays, and nothing is sent for it. A chase under way when the keypad goes dark ends at once: each button it had changed gets back, once, what it shows without the chase.
  • The Breeze animation: no gust plays, and nothing is sent for it. Gusts under way end the same way, and Breeze ending while the keypad is dark plays no ending wipe.
  • The steady LEDs still follow the fan, because they are its state: the selected speed's button lights and the others go dark at a new speed, and Breeze starting or ending changes the speed buttons as it does without the animation. The keypad shows them by its own profile (nothing, in the dark, with a minimum of 0), and they are right the moment the room is lit.
  • Resuming starts afresh; nothing missed is replayed. The first chase comes 2.5 seconds after the resume, as after a new speed, and while the fan blows a breeze the gusts start again as when Breeze begins.

The driver never imitates the keypad's dimming: it sends no "off", no black and no dimmed colour in place of the dark. It only stops sending the animations.

What the connection does to the keypad. While it is connected, the keypad's ambient light Sensing reads Source instead of Independent. It still uses its own sensor, and its own LEDs dim exactly as before; disconnecting it makes it Independent again. The keypad then sends its reading over Zigbee when it changes: nothing while the room's light is steady, a few messages a minute while it flickers, far fewer than one chase sends. The driver never reads or changes the keypad's profile, so the threshold is a row of its own: if you change the keypad's Dark Room, change Pause LED Animations Below to match.

Limits.

  • One keypad. The input takes one keypad's reading, and all the speed buttons pause by it: a second keypad on the speed links, in another room, loses its animations while the connected keypad is dark and keeps them while it is lit, however dark its own room is. Connect the keypad whose room matters most.
  • Nothing connected: the animations run exactly as before, whatever the light.
  • After a restart (a driver update, a Director restart, a controller reboot), and after the connection is made, the driver has no reading until the keypad's next change, and the animations run as before until then. A steady dark room may send nothing for a long time, so a chase can play in the dark until the light next changes; switching a lamp on and off makes the keypad report. The keypad's first message after the connection is made reads 0 whatever the light, and the driver ignores it.
  • A connection Control4 does not document for drivers. Neither the Ambient Light Level connection nor the message it carries is in Control4's documentation for drivers. What the driver relies on was measured on a Control4 keypad dimmer on OS 3.4.x; other keypads and OS 4.x have not been tried. A keypad or an OS that sends nothing over it pauses nothing: the animations run as before.
  • A minimum above 0. With the keypad's status LED Min Brightness above 0 its LEDs are only dimmed in the dark: the animations still pause below the threshold, but the selected speed's button stays faintly lit.
  • No controller reboot. The pause adds a connection and a property row only.

Turning the animations off

One step each, on the fan item's Properties tab, and it acts at once:

  • The speed chase: set Speed LED Animation to Off and click Set.
  • The Breeze gusts: set Breeze LED Animation to Off and click Set.

The speed buttons then follow the fan as any button on Follow Connection does: the button of the fan's speed lit, the others dark. There is no single switch for both, no programming command for it, and no way to show the rows without a speed button bound.

Setting Speed LED Colors to Keypad's own stops both as well, but leaves the keypad with the last colours it was sent (Speed LED Colors, chapter 7).

Offline

After a failed request the driver tries again after 10 seconds, then 20, then 40 and then once a minute; the third failure in a row makes the fan offline, about 45 seconds after it went quiet. It cannot tell a power cut at the wall from a stuck Wi-Fi module while the blades keep turning, and it does not guess: it keeps the last state, marked not current in Status, instead of reporting the fan as off. Control4's fan has no "offline" of its own, so the fan tile keeps showing the last speed, and the Breeze fan its last strength; use Status, the Online variable and the Fan Offline event, and the light's greyed-out controls. While the fan is offline the driver also looks for it on the network by its MAC address, in case its address has changed (chapter 12).

Coming back

The first answer after the silence starts a new first contact: the driver checks the fan's identity and firmware, reads its state and reports what differs from the last known state. Fan Online fires once and the fast polling (every 2 seconds for 20 seconds) starts. Nothing is re-sent, whatever Control4 last commanded: a fan stopped with the wall control's pull tab stays stopped, and a speed you sent while it was away is not delivered late.

Reboot Fan

For a Wi-Fi module that has stopped behaving, Reboot Fan (Actions tab) asks the fan to restart its Wi-Fi module. It works only while the fan answers, only on your click, and never automatically; it is not a programming command. The driver then waits up to two minutes (Status Rebooting the fan's Wi-Fi module (sent 14:02)), asking every 10 seconds; the first answer starts a first contact. After two minutes of silence the fan counts as offline. Last Action says:

  • Reboot sent to the fan's Wi-Fi module at 14:02
  • Reboot not sent: the fan is not answering (power it off for 10 s) while the fan is offline: a fan that does not answer cannot hear it; switch its power off at the wall for 10 seconds instead.
  • Reboot not sent: no fan of this device answers at 10.0.2.151 when no address is set or another fan, a Gen 4 fan or a non-fan answers.
  • Reboot already sent at 14:02; waiting for the fan while it is rebooting.

The wrong fan

The driver pins the first fan it meets. If a different Modern Forms fan answers at the address (a new lease handed the address to another fan, a fan was replaced, the wrong address was typed), the driver refuses to talk to it: Status reads Another fan (...5302) answers at 10.0.2.151; nothing is sent, commands are refused, keypad LEDs keep their state and Fan Offline fires. The pinned fan is not forgotten; the driver asks again every minute and carries on by itself if the right fan returns to that address, or, unless you typed that address yourself, follows it if it turns up at a new one (chapter 12). To use the other fan on purpose, see chapter 12.

Following a new address

Once the driver has learned the fan (chapter 3), the device belongs to that fan, not to an address. When the fan stops answering at Fan Address, or something else answers there, the driver asks the network for the fan by its MAC address, with the same multicast DNS question that fills Found Fans (chapter 3). If the fan announces itself at another address, the driver reads its identity there once, sends it nothing else, and moves only when the client id and the MAC address are the learned fan's. No click is needed.

  • When. The search starts when the driver counts the fan offline, about 45 seconds after it went quiet (three failed requests), or at once when another fan, a Gen 4 fan or a device that is not a fan answers at the address. The first look is 2 seconds later, plus up to about 25 seconds so that the devices of several fans do not all ask at the same moment; then after 30 seconds, 1, 2, 5 and 10 minutes, then every 15 minutes while the fan is away. Each look takes a few seconds. A fan that has moved is usually found again within a minute or two of going quiet. This search does not run while the fan answers, while it reboots, or while Fan Address is empty (the search at start-up that fills Found Fans is chapter 3's).
  • What you see. Status goes from Not answering at 10.0.2.151 since 14:02; showing last state to Waiting for the fan at 10.0.2.152 and then Online: …. Fan Address is rewritten with the new address, and Last Action reads Followed fan MF_00005E005301 to 10.0.2.152 (was 10.0.2.151) (also written to the log). Programming sees one Fan Offline and one Fan Online, as for any outage; the move has no event of its own, and nothing is re-sent to the fan. The device, its links, its programming and the names of its two items stay as they are. Each of these searches also refreshes Found Fans, which then lists the fan at its new address, but the driver never takes anything from that list by itself. A look that finds nothing changes nothing and writes nothing in Last Action.
  • An address you type wins. After you click Set on Fan Address, the driver follows the fan nowhere else until the fan has answered at the address you typed. A typing mistake, another fan's address or an address for later therefore stays as you typed it, and Status says what answers there. Typing the address the fan was last seen at is not such a change: the driver carries on as before. An empty Fan Address stops everything, following included, and the driver never fills it in.
  • Only this fan, only nearby. The driver follows only the fan it has learned: never another fan, never by name or model, and only to a private or link-local address (the kind a home network uses) that the fan itself announced. It never scans the network. Multicast DNS stays on the controller's own network segment, so a fan on another subnet, VLAN or guest Wi-Fi, or behind an access point that filters multicast, is not found; type its address instead.
  • Too many moves. When the fan would move a fourth time within an hour, the driver does not follow it, says so once (below) and follows again once the hour is over. An address that changes that often, or two devices answering for one fan, needs a look at the router.
  • Switching it off. Set Follow Address Changes to Off and click Set (chapter 14). A fan at a new address then stays "not answering" until you type its address. The driver then searches only at start-up, so Found Fans shows the fan at its new address only after a restart or an update.
  • Tried so far. The fan's answer that following relies on has been seen from Gen 3 fans only. Gen 1 and 2 fans are expected to answer the same way, but that has not been tried.

13. The fan's own automations

These fans can run things themselves: schedules and sleep timers set in the Modern Forms app, Away mode (random on and off), adaptive learning. The driver does not fight them or hide them. It shows them, says when a change was not made by Control4, and offers two clicks.

  • Fan Automations reads None, or Active: and the items that apply, joined by commas: schedule, away mode, adaptive learning, fan timer 25 min, light timer 10 min (Gen 3 fans report the time left; minutes are rounded up) or fan timer set, light timer set (Gen 1 and 2). When the row would be too long for Composer, away mode and adaptive learning are shortened to away and learning. It is rewritten when the text changes.
  • Last Change By is written whenever the fan changed and Control4 did not do it, for example Remote or wall control at 14:05. Gen 3 fans say where a change came from, and the driver words it: Remote or wall control, Modern Forms app or another local client, Modern Forms cloud (app away, voice assistant), The fan's sleep timer, The fan's away mode, A schedule in the Modern Forms app. When the fan does not say (Gen 1 and 2 fans, or a source it does not name), it reads Outside Control4 (source not reported). What a given remote reports has not been checked on every model.
  • Turn Off Away Mode and Turn Off Adaptive Learning (Actions tab) switch those two off. They never switch them on; the Modern Forms app can turn them on again. Last Action reads Away mode turned off (the Modern Forms app can turn it on again) or Adaptive learning turned off (the Modern Forms app can turn it on); Away mode is already off or Adaptive learning is already off if nothing was needed; Not changed: the fan is not answering; and The fan did not take it; it still reports away mode on (… adaptive learning on) if the fan refused.
  • Schedules and timers are never set or cleared by the driver. Clear them in the Modern Forms app, and put the automation in Control4 programming instead, where it can be seen with the rest of the house.

16. Limits

  • Fans: Modern Forms generations 1 to 3 with six speeds. A generation 4 fan is refused; a fan with fewer speeds is not supported. One device controls one fan; several fans are several devices.
  • No push: the fan reports nothing, so everything made outside Control4 is seen at the next poll (15 seconds by default).
  • The fan's own automations: schedules, sleep timers, Away mode and adaptive learning are shown but never set or cleared, apart from the two turn-off clicks. UV-C and other special features are not controlled.
  • Breeze: on Gen 3 fans that have it, switched and set from programming, the Fan Breeze link and the Breeze fan. The fan's tile cannot show it (it shows off in Breeze mode), and its gusts are not shown as speeds. That the fan runs in fan mode or in Breeze mode, never both, was seen on the owner's fan; whether its remote's power button keeps Breeze set, and which direction it turns in Breeze, has not been tried: the driver shows what the fan reports.
  • The light's fades are a staircase of about 7 to 18 steps over 3 seconds, because the fan takes one request at a time; a fade of under about 1 second or over fewer than 3 levels is one request; the Navigator's slider jumps; a change made in the Modern Forms app or the remote during a short fade may be overwritten by the next step, while an off from outside ends the fade and is never undone; a failed step ends the fade; nothing resumes after a restart (Fades, chapter 6). An update from release 1.4 or earlier needs one controller reboot for the Click Rates rows (chapter 3). Light Min On is a property of the fan device, not Control4's Range Levels, and there is no Max On; with it set, the Modern Forms app's percentages differ from Control4's, and a level below it set outside Control4 shows as 1 % (Light Min On, chapter 6). No colour and no colour temperature on the light.
  • Address: only a dotted IPv4 address, no host names. The driver follows the fan it has learned to a new address only when the fan announces itself on the controller's own network segment, only to a private or link-local address, and only after the fan has answered there as itself; an address you typed wins until the fan has answered at it (chapter 12). It never scans the network and never takes another fan by itself. The search that fills Found Fans asks one multicast DNS question on the controller's own network segment, by itself: at every start-up, and on a schedule while the device has no fan yet. Following relies on an answer seen from Gen 3 fans only so far.
  • Keypads: a bound fan button shows the keypad's own LED colours unless Speed LED Colors is Per speed. Double taps are keypad programming. No hold on the fan links. The Breeze animation and the speed chase play on the speed links only, need Per speed, and order the buttons by speed number, not by where they sit on the keypad. The speed chase repeats every rest for as long as the fan runs at a fixed speed, and while it plays the speed buttons do not show the speed (chapter 10). The keypad LED rows are shown only while a device is bound to a Fan Speed link, and the driver cannot tell a keypad with colour LEDs from another device bound there. The pause in the dark follows one keypad's reading, through a connection Control4 does not document for drivers, tried only with a Control4 keypad dimmer on OS 3.4.x (chapter 10).
  • Programming: four events, two variables, two conditions and four Breeze commands of the driver's own; a Reboot Fan, a turn-off or Add Breeze Fan is a click, never programmed; no "changed outside Control4" event, no History entries.
  • Devices: the Breeze fan is a device of its own, added only with Add Breeze Fan, with no keypad links, no direction and no offline state of its own; the fan device stays as it is and nothing needs to be added again. The Breeze switch of release 1.2 cannot be turned into a Breeze fan: it is deleted and the Breeze fan added (chapter 9).
  • Icons: the driver's own fan and light icons. A device keeps the icons of the build that created it, so a device added with build 73 or earlier keeps Control4's own fan and light icons after an update; only removing and adding the device again would change them, and that loses its connections and programming.
  • Detecting a second device for the same fan: not done; add one device per fan.
  • A restored project: the identity is expected to be found again from Fan Address and Fan ID and checked at the first contact, and a fan that moved since the backup to be followed (chapter 12); the light's keypad settings may fall back to their defaults. This has not been tried yet.
  • Controllers: Control4 OS 3.3.0 or newer. Tried on OS 3.4.x only.

17. Privacy and security

Privacy

The driver talks only to the fan on your network and to nothing outside the house. To find fans (the Found Fans list, chapter 3, and following a fan that moved, chapter 12) it also asks, by itself, one multicast DNS question that stays on your local network: at every start-up, and on a schedule while the device has no fan yet or is following its fan. It never scans the network. It reads the fan's state and identity: on, speed, direction, light on and level, Breeze and its strength, Away mode, adaptive learning, whether a schedule or a timer is set, the model, the firmware, the client id and the MAC address.

The fan also hands out details that belong to you, and the driver drops them the moment they arrive: the owner's e-mail address, the cloud identity, the fan's name from the app, the Wi-Fi network's name and address, the time zone, the cloud certificate and the schedule itself (it only notes whether one exists). They are never stored, never shown in a property and never logged. This differs from the stock driver, whose rows show some of them.

The client id and MAC address are shown in the Fan ID row, in your own Composer, because you need them for the router; in the log they appear only as their last four digits. Before you share a screenshot of the Properties tab, hide Fan ID and Fan Address.

Security

The fan's local interface has no password and no encryption: anything on your network can control the fan, with or without this driver, and the driver cannot add protection the fan does not have. Keep the fan on a network you trust. The identity the driver pins (chapter 3) keeps it from controlling the wrong fan by mistake; it is a check, not a password, since the fan announces its client id and MAC address in the clear.

The driver sends requests only to the fan you set up, and, while it follows that fan, to a private or link-local address that announced itself as that fan (chapter 12), besides its one multicast DNS question; it never contacts the internet. The input Ambient Light Level (Follower) takes only light readings from the keypad connected to it, and they can only pause the LED animations. Do not report a security problem in a public issue: the project's security policy on GitHub says how to report it privately.

18. Where to follow the work

The project, its plans, its contracts and its issues are on GitHub: github.com/corytron/c4-modernforms-driver. The changes of every release are listed at the end of this page.
